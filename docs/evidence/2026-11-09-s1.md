# S1: crossed cylinders, end to end

Plan step `proving-ground.s1` ([the milestone](../plan/proving-ground.md)). The code is throwaway, on branch `spike-s1`, in
`spikes/s1/`. Nothing here is copied into a crate (L51). Raw rows and the commands are in `spikes/s1/results/`.

## Question

Taking two crossing cylinders, a sphere cut by a box and a NURBS face cut by a plane from surfaces to a watertight solid, what
do the topology and the tolerance data really have to hold?

## What was measured

**The prototype.** A small but real curved boolean, about 5,000 lines with its test harness, built as six phases with data passed between them:
broad phase, pair intersection, edge split, face split, classify, stitch. It handles plane, cylinder, sphere and bicubic
B-spline faces. A cylinder's side is two patches and a sphere two charts, so no face has a seam or a pole. Sections are closed
forms for the analytic pairs, and marching squares refined by Newton for plane against NURBS. Methods come from published
sources: Levin (CACM 19(10), 1976) and Miller (ACM TOG 6(4), 1987) for the cylinder-quadric section; Piegl and Tiller, *The
NURBS Book*, for evaluation; Hoschek and Lasser (1993) for closest points; Lorensen and Cline (SIGGRAPH 1987) for cell-wise
contouring; Mäntylä (1988) for half-edge loop walking; Roth (1982) for ray-parity classification; Sutherland and Hodgman (CACM
17(1), 1974) for polygon clipping; Abramowitz and Stegun 25.4.29 for quadrature; Ahlfors for the stereographic chart. Open
CASCADE was used only as a black box, through its result files.

**Checks.** Every result is measured from its own boundary: volume and area by the divergence and Green's theorems along the
trim curves, Euler characteristic `V - E + 2F - L`, edges used exactly twice in opposite directions, the net flux of the
boundary normal, and the largest distance from any edge to the surfaces of its faces (the *gap*). Against that:

- closed-form volume, area and Euler where the corpus case has them;
- an independent volume for the near-tangent families, computed here without the kernel: a graded Gauss-Legendre integral for
  crossed cylinders, and the spherical cap for sphere against box;
- the volume identity `V(A) + V(B) = V(A union B) + V(A intersect B)` across each group, to 1e-6 of the largest volume (L11);
- agreement with the Open CASCADE 8.0.1 oracle, geometrically and by tolerance (L12). Only agree/disagree flags are kept in the
  result files: no value computed by the oracle is committed.

An answer is *wrong* if it fails a closed-form or independent check. *Open* means an edge not used exactly twice, or a net flux
larger than a slit ten tolerances wide along every edge would leave. A *report* is a failure returned as an
`occam_report::Report` (a copy of the crate with this spike's codes added), with the input unchanged. *Not supported* is an
operation the prototype has no code for.

**Cases.** The 546 cases of `tools/corpus/generated/`; and three generated families with known answers, 120 NURBS blocks and 60
crossed-cylinder pairs, each run as common, cut and fuse:

- `nurbs-vertical` and `nurbs-corner`: a bicubic graph cut by one or two vertical planes. Exact polynomial volumes.
- `nurbs-horizontal` and `nurbs-near-peak`: cut by a horizontal plane, the latter `1e-1` to `1e-9` mm below the highest point.
  Volume by a fine 2-D rule, checked only to 5e-5 of the block, which is loose.
- `crossed-random*`: skew axes at random angles and offsets, a third near external tangency and a third near internal. No
  closed form: checked by point sampling (2,000,000 samples, to five standard errors with a floor of ten sample cells) and by the
  two volume identities. The sampling cannot see a small lens, so the identities and the gap carry those cases.

**Held out.** The random families were debugged on seeds 11 and 21 (NURBS) and 3 and 5 (crossed). After every fix was frozen they were run once on fresh seeds
(9901 and 7701), and whatever failed there was left as it is.

**Tolerance.** One model `Tolerance`, 1e-7 mm and 1e-9 rad, with a sweep from 1e-5 to 1e-9 mm. **Removal experiments:** switches
in `spikes/s1/src/toggles.rs` take one piece of data or one rule out of the kernel, to see what it was needed for.

**Speed** was measured through `spikes/tools/remote.sh` on an Intel Core i7-8700K, 6 physical cores, governor `powersave`, rustc
1.99.0, `RUSTFLAGS` empty, target `x86_64-unknown-linux-gnu`, one thread pinned with `taskset -c 0`, 7 repeats, boolean phases
only (no measuring). Everything else ran locally. S1 is not a parallel investigation and reports no speed-up.

The families this spike is about (near-tangent crossed cylinders, near-tangent sphere against box, sphere caps and lenses,
Steinmetz) are 362 cases: all 362 answered, none wrong. Against closed form and the independent volumes the median relative
volume error in every family is 2.5e-10 or better. The worst single case used 2.4% of its tolerance (1e-6 of the largest input).
A near-tangent lens with almost no volume can still have a relative error near 1 and pass: its absolute error is tiny, and this
check cannot judge it.

**Every disagreement with the oracle on volume went our way.** In all 71 the prototype passes the closed-form or independent
check and the oracle's value is outside that check's tolerance (65 near-tangent crossed cylinders, 6 sphere cases). All 18 near-tangent groups of each family satisfy the volume identity here, against 29 of 72
groups that fail it in the oracle's own results (16 internal, 13 near-Steinmetz), which matches the brief's warning not to use
it as ground truth there. The 21 Euler disagreements are in near-tangent families with no closed-form Euler, and are not
adjudicated.

The 32 reports are all one cause (below). The 46 not supported are cones, tori and fillets: those families were not attempted.

### How far edges stray from their faces

The gap is the largest distance from a sampled point of an edge to the surface of either of its faces. In 14 corpus results and
27 of 60 random near-external operations it is above the model tolerance, by up to 140 times (1.4e-5 mm against 1e-7 mm).

The size matches a loss of half the digits at the turning points of a section curve, where the discriminant goes to zero and its
square root is about `sqrt(eps) * L`. To test that, a switch (`S1_RECENTRE`) moves the section's axial origin to the foot of the
common perpendicular, so `L` becomes the radius rather than half the cylinder's length:

| run | results over tolerance | worst gap (mm) | wrong answers |
|---|---|---|---|
| corpus, as built | 14 | 1.9e-6 | 0 |
| corpus, re-based | 8 | 3.4e-7 | **6** |
| random, as built | 27 | 1.4e-5 | 0 |
| random, re-based | 18 | 8.6e-7 | 0 |

So the origin explains most of the size (6 and 16 times smaller), and what remains is still above the tolerance, about what
`sqrt(eps) * radius` predicts. **It is not a free fix:** it made 6 exact Steinmetz results wrong (open boundaries, volumes off by
between 0.5% and 134%), where the same cases were right before. Why was not isolated; those are the cases with true pinch points, where the zero
test on the discriminant is decided at rounding level. The recorded results all use the code as built, without the switch.

### What a failure could hand back (L5)

Every failure returned a `Report` and left the input untouched: the inputs are borrowed and immutable, so this holds by
construction. Of the fields asked for:

| field | filled in? |
|---|---|
| Failing face pair | Yes for pair failures, and one face for split failures. `stitch-open` names no face, because the failure is global |
| Curves found | As a count only (`section_curves`, `section_pieces`, `curves_found`). `Report` has no way to carry the geometry of a curve |
| Residual gap | Only where the code computes one: `hit-not-on-curve`, `dangling-section`, `stitch-open`. `coincident-surfaces` and `loop-not-closed` have none |
| Phase reached, candidate pairs, hits, regions | Always |
| Last valid model | A placeholder. The prototype has no model ids, so it can only say "the input" |
| Which other pairs would have failed | Never: phases stop at the first failure, so one report describes one pair |

The final runs produced only three codes (`coincident-surfaces`, `dangling-section`, `loop-not-closed`), so those are the ones
counted. `hit-not-on-curve`, `pair-failed` and `classify-inconsistent` were seen while building. `stitch-open`, `split-not-tiling`,
`classify-ambiguous` and `unsupported` never fired, and what they would hand back is read from the code only.

The `Report` entity ids are positions in this run's arrays, not stable ids. Its values are numbers only, so a reason such as "this
surface pair has no section" could not be said, and the prototype used the message's length as a placeholder for it.

### Where `occam-units` got in the way

Vertices hold `Point<World>` and the model holds `Tolerance`, which were both fine. But `occam-units` has no dot, cross, norm or
unit vector, so every formula in the prototype drops to raw `[f64; 3]`. Surface derivatives have no type: a cylinder's `Su` is
millimetres per radian and a NURBS's is millimetres per knot unit. The typed layer stopped at the edge of the geometry code.

## Answer

### What the topology has to hold

Each item is backed by a case that needed it.

1. **Vertices shared by identity, merged by the model tolerance as they are created.** Removing the merge adds 24
   `dangling-section` failures.
2. **For each face, the surface and which sheet of a periodic parameter it lives on.** Removing that adds 152 failures and 20
   wrong answers, the most damaging removal measured.
3. **Each face's loops, with which is the outer one, established by containment of a strict interior point and not by area
   alone.** The bug that came from assuming otherwise broke 52 cases. A loop is the reversal of another loop at every section
   edge, so the relation is not optional.
4. **For each edge, the two uses (face and direction).** The check that every edge is used exactly twice in opposite directions,
   and that labels differ across exactly the section edges, needs this adjacency. It caught the 52-case bug before any geometry
   was built.
5. **Real seam edges on periodic surfaces, with the surface cut into chart-sized patches, and no poles.** Every cylinder is two
   patches and every sphere two charts, and no case needed a face to cross its own seam. Whether that beats a seam edge used twice
   was not compared.
6. **Four-valent pinch vertices where a section curve crosses itself.** Without them the four exact Steinmetz cuts come back as 1
   shell instead of 2, with volumes unchanged and no check failing. Nothing but the topology records the difference.
7. **A way to hold a section curve's parameter image on a NURBS.** Not for correctness: without it all 180 operations gave the
   same answers. But the boolean phases are 3.45 times slower, because every use becomes a closest-point search.

### What the tolerance data has to hold

8. **One model tolerance was enough to make every decision in these cases.** Between 1e-5 and 1e-9 mm the answers do not change,
   apart from 2 reports when an offset equals the tolerance. No operation needed a tolerance on a vertex: the prototype's per-vertex
   `tol` field is written and copied and never read. That is evidence for S2, not a decision. It holds at this size (models up to
   about 100 mm) and for these operations.
9. **Each edge needs a measured deviation from its faces, and the model tolerance cannot be asserted in its place.** In 45% of the
   near-external random crossings (35% on held-out seeds) an edge lies further from its face than the model tolerance, by up to 140
   times, while every volume is right. Better conditioning shrinks this but did not remove it and broke other cases, as above.
10. **A sampled curve needs a stated fit error.** Refinement that did not measure its own fit broke 33 NURBS operations, and the
    prototype's curves now carry the achieved fit (at most 2e-8 mm). Pinning the curve's ends exactly to the boundary roots made no
    difference over 360 operations, so that is not on this list.
11. **A tolerance has to apply to depth, not only to width.** A near-tangent section loop can be about 4e-3 mm across and still be
    thinner than a 1e-7 mm tolerance. Removing the rule changes one case only, so the evidence is weak.
12. **Zero tests have to scale with the rounding of the value being tested**, not only with the length tolerance. One exact Steinmetz
    cut was wrong without it, and the re-basing experiment shows how sensitive those same cases remain.
13. **The broad phase has to be conservative**, because a pair it drops is a silent wrong answer that the volume identity cannot see.

Answer in one line: the topology needs shared vertices, sheet-aware faces, ordered loops, and edge uses; the tolerance data needs
one model tolerance plus a measured deviation per edge and a fit error per sampled curve.

## What this does not show

- That the prototype is a kernel. It is small, single-threaded, and has no fillets, cones, tori or coincident-surface handling.
- That the fixes generalise. Most were found on the same cases that measure them. The held-out seeds are the only unseen test,
  and they exposed a 10% failure rate in `nurbs-near-peak` that the debugged seeds had hidden.
- That the volume checks are sharp on small features. The tolerance is 1e-6 of the largest input, so a thin lens can be wrong in
  relative terms and pass. The independent integrals exist only for the perpendicular near-tangent families, the skew ones are
  checked by sampling and identities, and the horizontal NURBS cuts only to 5e-5 of the block.
- Rational NURBS, NURBS against anything but a plane, or surfaces with interior `C0` knots. The B-spline block used non-rational
  bicubics only, and the plane is the only tool that cuts the NURBS face.
- That tessellation is watertight. The `tess-*` cases were measured as solids and no mesh was built.
- How any of this behaves on large models or at scales far from 1 to 100 mm.
- Which of the 21 Euler disagreements with the oracle are ours.
- Anything about parallel speed-up.

## What it decides

Nothing is decided here: this is evidence for `proving-ground.design` and the tolerance model in S2. It supports these inputs:

- Build `occam-topo` around shared vertices, sheet-aware faces, ordered loops and edge uses.
- S2 should test a per-edge measured deviation against one model tolerance, since the per-vertex field was never used here but the
  edges do exceed the model tolerance.
- Keep a measured deviation on section-curve edges rather than relying on conditioning alone, and a fit error on sampled curves.
- Coincident surfaces are the largest remaining failure class and need their own spike or step.

## Open questions for the user

- Should coincident surfaces (coplanar and coaxial-equal, the 32 reports) be in scope for a follow-up to S1, or wait for the boolean
  design? No decision is needed to use this finding.
- O6 (whether oracle output may be committed) stays open. This finding commits only agreement counts.
