# `proving-ground.s2`: the tolerance model

Evidence, frozen when written. Branch `spike-s2`, code in `spikes/s2/`, raw output in `spikes/s2/results/`, the definitions
fixed before measuring in `spikes/s2/QUESTION.md`. `spikes/s2/local.sh`, `spikes/s2/measure.sh` and `spikes/s2/quiet-window.sh`
(a command given to `spikes/tools/remote.sh`) produce the results, and `spikes/s2/summarise.py` turns most of them into the tables
here. Files named `-earlier-quiet-run` are the speed runs from commit `06cbf61`, kept because the machine was quieter then.

## Question

The same near-tangent inputs through (a) per-entity growing tolerances, (b) one model precision with certified intervals, and
(c) floating-point filters with an exact fallback. Which model should occam use, and what must `Tolerance` become?

## What was measured

Not a boolean (that is `proving-ground.s1`): only the decisions in one where a tolerance matters, each reduced to the sign of a
polynomial in the inputs and written once over a `Ring` trait, so every model evaluates the same formula.

| Name | Model | What it stores | Where the tolerance lives |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `a-local` | (a), after Jackson (1995) | `f64` position and one radius | On the entity. Compares with the sum of radii, which for fresh entities is `eps`. Merges grow it |
| `a-local-residual` | (a) in chains | The same | Grown by the measured incidence residual at each new vertex |
| `a-local-forward` | (a) in chains | The same | Grown by a first-order forward error estimate, and by the half-chord at a band decision |
| `b-interval` | (b), midpoint-radius (Rump 1999, Moore) | `f64` midpoint and radius per coordinate | One `eps` for the model. Otherwise `Undecided` |
| `b-interval-snap` | (b), chains only | The same | As `b-interval`, but a certified band decision places one vertex at the foot, with no hull of the two roots |
| `c0` | (c), Shewchuk (1997) | `f64` only | None. Band zero: exact sign |
| `c1` | (c) | `f64` only | One `eps`, compared exactly |
| `c1-interval-filter` | (c) with an interval as filter | `f64` only | As `c1`, filtering with a certified interval instead of the running-error "permanent" |
| `c0-tower`, `c1-tower` | (c), exact constructions (Yap 1997) | A tower of quadratic extensions | As `c0` and `c1` |

In chains, `c0-exact-pred` and `c1-exact-pred+eps` are `c0` and `c1` with exact predicates over vertices rounded to `f64`.

**Decisions.** D1: does a curve exist, or for internal and Steinmetz pairs which side of the tangency the pair is on. D2: the
shape of the curve at a near-singular place (one loop, two loops, or within `eps` of touching), canonical frames only. D3: do
two computations reach the same vertex. D4: a point against a surface. D5: do two points merge.

**Failure** is no answer: `Undecided`, a NaN, or the exact representation's work budget spent. **Wrong** is a confident answer
that the exact reference contradicts: a definite sign that is the wrong one or that is given when the quantity is exactly
zero, or `near` when `|q| > eps`. A definite answer with the right sign but `|q| <= eps` is a **sliver**: not wrong, but a claim
below the model's resolution. Each is judged against two references computed in big-integer arithmetic that shares nothing with
the expansions: **literal** (the numbers the model was given) and **nominal** (the numbers before any rounding).

**Inputs.** The corpus's near-tangent groups (72 groups, 162 decisions). A harder sweep generated here: 4170 instances and 153
point pairs, 8892 decisions, with gaps from 1e-2 down to 3e-16, zero, negative, and within eight units in the last place of
`eps` itself ("the band edge"), at scales 1, 128 and 8192, in canonical and three rotated frames, translated away from the
origin and with non-orthogonal axes. `eps` is 1e-7 mm unless stated, and was swept at 1e-5 and 1e-9.

**Composition.** A construction chain places a vertex, cuts a sphere centred on it with a line that nearly touches it, and takes
one intersection as the next vertex, 16 times. The steps come from Pythagorean integers, so the exact nominal chain is known
and every decision has an exact reference: five gap families (`clear`, about 1e-3; `around-eps`; `fine`, 7e-13 to 1e-9;
`tangent`, exactly zero; `mixed`), four chains each, 320 steps per model. Run both in the canonical frame, where IEEE arithmetic
happens to be exact for most steps, and under a rational rotation that makes the inputs round, as in the corpus's rotated
groups. Towers run in the canonical frame only. A merge chain feeds in 120 points within noise, with a decoy 50.5 `eps` away. A
two-route test reaches the same nominal vertex by two orders of the same steps.

**Machine.** Speed was measured through `spikes/tools/remote.sh` on an Intel Core i7-8700K, 6 physical cores, governor
`powersave`, `rustc` 1.99.0,
empty `RUSTFLAGS`, `x86_64-unknown-linux-gnu`, pinned with `taskset`, hyperthreads unused. Single-core turbo is higher than
all-core turbo, so speed-ups read a little low. Minimum of 7 to 9 repetitions is reported, each sized to at least about 120 ms
of wall time. The machine was not always quiet: other work was running on it during the last runs, which its load average
shows (see Cost). Everything else ran on a development machine and does not depend on it.

## Results

### The predicates are correct

Every certified sign was checked against the exact reference on moderate, cancelling, full-exponent-range and truly tangent
inputs (`results/checks.txt`).

| Backend | Signs it decided | Wrong signs |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Interval | 2,166,636 | 0 |
| Filter (permanent) | 2,145,563 | 0 |
| Expansion | 2,500,000 | 0 |

The filter has a guard against underflow, added from reasoning before the checks ran, and the checks were not run without it.
Expansions were tested in the moderate range only, since Dekker's splitting is not exact on overflow or underflow.

**Determinism (L23).** Digests of all answers are identical at 1, 2, 4 and 6 threads, in the decision tables and the speed runs
(`results/pairs-*`, `results/speed-*`). `Lanes<W>` for widths 2, 4 and 8 is bit-identical to scalar for intervals and filters,
over 10,000 rounds per width, with no mismatches (`results/checks.txt`). Expansions are not run in lanes.

`c1-interval-filter` gives the same answers as `c1`, as a test on all 4170 instances asserts. At `eps` = 1e-5 and 1e-9 the shape
is the same, except that at 1e-9 `b-interval` fails 11.1% inside the band and 35.2% at exact tangency. The likely reason is that
the band is then close to what its intervals resolve at scale 8192, but the failures were not broken down by scale.

- Away from the band all models are right.
- **Inside the band**, `a`, `b` and `c1` say near, and `c0` makes a sliver of almost everything (2990 of 3695). `c0` is also
  wrong against nominal in 10.5%: a rotated copy's rounding is larger than the gap, and it trusts the rounded numbers.
- **At exact tangency**, `c0` is confidently wrong against nominal in 68% of decisions, because a rotated exact tangency is no
  longer exactly tangent in `f64`. The models with a band are right.
- **At the band edge**, `a-local` is wrong against its own numbers in 21%, because plain floating point cannot tell.
  `b-interval`
  declines 91%. `c1` is exact on its numbers and 15% wrong against nominal, the rotation having moved the input across the band.
  These inputs are the knife edge on purpose.
- A rotated copy gets a different D1 answer from the canonical one in 8.1% of cases for `a-local`, 1.2% for `b-interval`, 20.5%
  for `c0` and 7.9% for `c1` (2979 pairs).

The filter's weak spot is the square of a small difference of large terms. The permanent of `q^2` is about `(2r)^2` even when
`q` is small, so 6.0% of decisions on easy inputs (gaps of 1e-4 and up) still reached the exact stage, all of them
point-against-
plane decisions (`results/fallback-rates.txt`). A midpoint-radius interval tracks the actual error and decides all of them:

| Set | Share reaching the exact stage: `c0` | `c1` (permanent) | `c1-interval-filter` |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Easy (gap at least 1e-4) | 6.0% | 6.0% | 0.0% |
| Hard (gap at most 1e-13) | 89.2% | 13.9% | 0.0% |
| The whole adversarial sweep | 37.0% | 31.8% | 22.7% |

**Against the OCCT oracle**, by the emptiness of the intersection only, counts and no values (`results/oracle.txt`). On the
external-cylinder and sphere-box families, where emptiness checks D1, `a`, `b`, `c1` and `c1-interval-filter` agree on all 26
non-empty groups (13 each), call 8 groups near that the oracle returns empty (4 each), and in 2 groups (1 each, gap less than
three times `eps`) say the surfaces cross where the oracle returns empty. Exact arithmetic on the input numbers says those two
overlap. No model says empty where the oracle finds a result, but none of these 36 groups is separated by a small gap, so an
"apart" answer is not tested. `c0` makes the same 26 agreements and says "cross" on all 10 groups the oracle returns empty. On
the internal and Steinmetz families the intersection is non-empty on both sides of the tangency, so emptiness cannot check D1,
and no comparison was made. The oracle is not ground truth here (L11): it fails the volume identity in 29 of the 72 groups, in
the internal and Steinmetz families, and closed forms and exact signs decide.

### Composition: merges

| Scenario | Model | Stored radius at the end | False merge | Clusters |
| --- | --- | ---: | --- | ---: |
| Drift, 0.9 `eps` a step, decoy at 50.5 `eps` | `a-local` | 54.55 `eps` | step 54 | 1 |
| | `b-interval`, `c1` | none | none | 60 |
| | `c0` | none | none | 120 |
| Noise of 0.5 `eps`, no drift | `a-local` | 1.66 `eps` | none | 1 |
| | `b-interval`, `c1` | none | none | 1 |
| | `c0` | none | none | 120 |

Jackson's growth does what it is said to do: under drift the ball grows without bound and swallows a feature 50 `eps` away.
Without a stored radius, merges within `eps` of a fixed representative cannot drift. They produce 60 vertices where a person
would say one. That is the honest answer for points that really are spread over 107 `eps`.

At `eps` = 1e-5 and 1e-9, `b-interval` still fails 85% of steps and is never wrong, `b-interval-snap` fails 13% and 44% and is
wrong against nominal in 18% and 8%, and the others are wrong against nominal in 15 to 44% (`results/chains-rotated-*`). In
the canonical frame, where most steps round to nothing, the wrong-against-nominal counts are
lower for the `f64` models, and the towers' are `c0-tower` 0 and `c1-tower` 35 of 240 (`results/chains-eps1e-7.txt`). The
failure
rate of `b-interval` by step is 0, 60, 75, 80, 80, 80 and then 100%, and of `b-interval-snap` 0, 15, 25, 35, 35, 35, then 55 to
65%. It fails about as often as `b-interval` where steps really cross (`around-eps` 89%, `clear` 62%, `mixed` 66%), since the
amplification there is real, and far less where the steps are inside the band (`fine` 3%, `tangent` 0%), where a snapped vertex
keeps a tight interval.

- **Every model that keeps going is wrong on part of this, and the one that is never wrong stops.** At a near-tangent step a
  small move
  of the sphere's centre moves the root by `R/h` times as much, for half-chord `h`: 55 at a gap of 1e-3, thousands near the
  band.
  That amplification is real, not an artefact of any arithmetic.
- **(a) is confidently wrong.** With the residual rule, the stored radius stays near 1e-15 while the vertex is already 1e-5
  away,
  and it encloses the true vertex in only 146 of 320 steps. The forward rule encloses it in 290 of 320, but by depth 15 the
  radius
  is 450 mm, its band swallows real features, and 38% of its decisions are wrong against nominal.
- **(b) never lies.** `b-interval` made no wrong answer in 320 steps at any of the three `eps`, and at 1e-7 its enclosure
  held the
  true vertex in all 320. The price is a failure rate of 60% at the second step and 100% from the seventh, because a `near`
  at one step leaves the
  next one with a hull of about `sqrt(2 R eps)` around the foot, and intervals overestimate the true error further. At `clear`
  gaps the half-width is 1.2e-6 at depth 3 where the actual error is 1.9e-10.
- **`b-interval-snap` is (b) with a modelling choice.** Treating a certified band decision as one vertex at the foot halves the
  failures (44%) and makes 8% of decisions wrong against nominal, none against its own numbers. Those are the cost of a
  band, the
  same cost `c1` pays (25%), except that some of them surface as a failure instead.
- **(c) with rounded constructions is exact about the wrong numbers.** `c0-exact-pred` and `c1-exact-pred+eps` are never wrong
  against their own vertex, and never fail. They are wrong against nominal in 44% and 25% of decisions, and the vertex is
  0.06 mm
  off by depth 15 in the `clear` family. The exact predicate does nothing about the error the construction put there. An exact
  predicate on a rounded exactly-tangent step is wrong against nominal every time (64 of 64).
- **Exact constructions work until they do not.** Run in the canonical frame, `c0-tower` is right on every step that it
  finishes,
  and comparing the towers of the two routes gives the correct answer on all 24 pairs at depth 3 and at depth 4. (The

  (`results/tower-cost.txt`: family `mixed`, band zero, the median over 4 chains, with no reference or measuring in the count.)

**The near-tangent position shift.** A band decision moves the vertex by the half-chord `sqrt(2 R g)` of the gap it discarded,
not by the gap. For the `fine` family (gaps of 7e-13 to 1e-9, inside the band), `c1`'s vertex is 1.1e-5 mm from the true one
after one step, at a gap of 1e-11. At `eps` = 1e-7 and `R` = 6, a contact inside the band can sit 1.1e-3 mm from the point it
represents, four orders of magnitude more than `eps`. Every model with a band has this cost, and only (b)'s hull and (a)'s
forward rule say so.

## Answer

**A stated combination: `c1` for decisions, `b` for what is computed, one tolerance per model, and no growing radii.**

1. **One tolerance for the model**, compared exactly. It is a band: `|q| <= eps` means tangent, on, or same. It is never
   stored on
   an entity and never grows. The growing radius of (a) is confidently wrong under composition, and does not stay bounded.
2. **Decisions on stored numbers are exact**: a certified interval first, then expansions. On the sweep this gives no failures,
   and no wrong answer against the numbers it was given, for a cost given below. The running-error filter built here is both
  slower
   and weaker than the interval, so the filter stage should be the interval.
3. **A computed value carries a certified error radius**, produced by the interval arithmetic that built it. It is not a
   tolerance and it obeys no growth rule. A decision on a computed value uses the interval, and where the interval straddles
   the band the answer is "cannot decide at this resolution", a first-class outcome that carries the failure state of L5.
4. **A band decision at near tangency places one vertex at the foot, and the report records the position uncertainty**
   `sqrt(2 R eps)`, because that, not `eps`, is how far the vertex moved. This is `b-interval-snap`.
5. **Exact towers are an optional, depth-limited escalation**, not the default. They give the right answer where they
   finish, but
   their work grows about 5 times a step: one chain in four is past 4e9 limb multiplications at 12 steps, and comparing two
  routes
   of 6 steps is past it too.

What `occam_units::Tolerance` must become: it stays one value per model, `length` and `angle`, as today. It gains the meaning
written above, compared exactly, and a method for the near-tangent position uncertainty. The certified error radius of a
computed
value is a separate type on computed geometry, not a field of `Tolerance`. There is no per-entity tolerance. Foreign geometry
with its own stated accuracy is the one case where a per-entity number might be justified; this spike did not measure it (see
below).

What it costs against the alternatives:

- **Speed:** a decision on stored numbers costs 52 to 58 ns with an interval filter, against 6 to 8 ns for plain floating point
  and 42 to 45 ns for a bare interval. That is about 8 times plain `f64` per decision, and it buys no wrong answers on the
  numbers
  given. A step that constructs a vertex costs 91 ns with intervals against 12 ns in plain `f64`. Adversarial inputs on the band
  edge reach the exact stage at a few microseconds each.
- **Stored data:** 48 bytes per computed vertex (midpoints and radii), 32 if one radius per vertex is enough, against 32 for a
  position with a local tolerance and 24 for a bare position.
- **Failures and wrong answers**, for 16-step rotated near-tangent chains at `eps` = 1e-7: the combination
  (`b-interval-snap`) fails
  44% of steps and is wrong against nominal in 8%, never against its own numbers. Per-entity tolerances never fail and are wrong
  against nominal in 27 to 38%, and cracks appear in 14 of 24 two-route comparisons with the residual rule. Bare intervals are
  never wrong and fail 86%. Exact predicates over rounded vertices are wrong against nominal in 25 to 44%, and exact towers are
  right but reach about 1.3e9 limb multiplications by 12 steps.

## What this does not show

- **Spheres, cylinders, planes and lines only.** The decisions are polynomials of low degree. No NURBS, no trimmed faces, and no
  marching: what an interval costs on a curve-curve intersection, where the iteration itself must be enclosed, is not measured.
- **No boolean.** `proving-ground.s1` builds one. The chains are synthetic and deliberately hard: 16 near-tangent steps in a
  row,
  each amplifying the error. A real model has fewer such steps. The failure rate by depth says how many before the interval runs
  out, not how often that happens in practice.
- **The per-entity model is a reconstruction** from Jackson's published idea. Two growth rules were tried, and a kernel could do
  something else. Neither was tuned against the corpus.
- **Foreign geometry with its own accuracy**, as a STEP file brings, was not tested.
- **Towers ran only in the canonical frame**, and only eagerly: a lazy expression DAG with precision on demand, as in Yap's
  exact
  geometric computation, was not built. It might be cheaper, but near-tangent steps are the ones a filter cannot decide, so how
  much it would save is not known.
- **The running-error filter is one simple design.** A filter tuned to each predicate, as Shewchuk builds them, might narrow the
  gap to the interval. It was not tried.
- **Speed is scalar.** `Lanes` was checked for bit-identity, not for speed-up. Expansions allocate, and were not tried with an
  arena.
- **Band-edge rates are of inputs placed on the edge on purpose.** They say what each model does there, not how often it
  happens.
- **Against the oracle, only emptiness was compared**, on two of the four families.

## What it decides

Recommends for `proving-ground.design`: the tolerance model is the combination above. (a) is rejected on the evidence of
composition, and
(c) with exact constructions is rejected as the default on cost. D1 to D5 in the kernel should be written once over a numeric
trait, as here, and given the failure outcome from the start (L5). The filter stage of an exact predicate should be a certified
interval, not a running-error permanent, on the evidence of the fallback rates above.

`docs/DETERMINISM.md` holds as written: intervals by midpoint and radius need no change of rounding mode, and use only
operations
the contract allows.

## Open questions for the user

1. **Is "cannot decide at this resolution" acceptable as a normal outcome of a kernel operation?** The combination depends
   on it.
   In near-tangent chains it is the answer in about 44% of decisions (`b-interval-snap`), and a kernel that must always return
   a solid needs a policy on top. Recommendation: yes, reported with its failure state.
2. **The default `eps`.** Everything here is relative to 1e-7 mm, swept to 1e-5 and 1e-9. At 1e-9 the intervals begin to run out
   of room at a scale of 8192. Recommendation: choose it with `proving-ground.s1`'s results in hand.
