# Finding: `proving-ground.p6`, what does robustness cost in parallel?

Code: `spikes/p6/` on branch `spike-p6`. Raw output is in `spikes/p6/results/`. `spikes/p6/run/all.sh`, given to
`spikes/tools/remote.sh`, produces `results/run1-raw.txt`; `spikes/p6/run/local.sh` produces the counts; `spikes/p6/summarise.py`
turns the raw output into the tables here. `spikes/p6/run/plain.sh` produces the plain `f64` comparator (`results/plain.txt`).
It builds on `spikes/s2/` (the decisions, by path) and `spikes/p11/` (the pool, by path), and the limits of both carry over.

## Question

S2 settled the shape of a robust decision: exact on stored numbers, by an interval filter then expansions. A decision costs 52
to 58 ns against 6 to 8 for plain `f64`. What does that cost in a parallel run: how much a lane-wide filter certifies, what
fallback costs, whether hard work piles up on one thread, and what fixes it?

## What was measured

**Decisions.** S2's D1 (does an intersection exist) and D4 (a point against a surface), from `spikes/s2/src/decide.rs`, over
the literal input numbers, band `eps` = 1e-7 mm unless stated. One item is two decisions. The filter is S2's
midpoint-radius interval (Rump, BIT 39, 1999) and the exact path S2's expansions (Shewchuk, DCG 18, 1997), as cited in
S2's finding; the papers were not re-read for this spike. Lane form
(`spikes/p6/src/decide.rs`) gathers `W` items of one family (external, internal, Steinmetz, sphere), evaluates D1 and D4 once
over `I<Lanes<W>>`, and decides each lane separately. A lane the interval cannot certify goes alone to the exact path. Width 1
is the scalar `I<f64>` path. The batch's items are grouped by family inside each chunk, so lanes stay homogeneous; leftovers
run scalar. The lane form's answers agree with the scalar form's (`spikes/p6/src/decide.rs`, three tests, and the digests below).

**Batches.** Items are drawn at random (a fixed SplitMix64 seed per batch name) from pools of unique geometry and repeated, so
each batch is many indices into a small table of geometry. Pools: `easy` (S2's sweep, gap at least 1e-4: 664 items), `corpus` (the
72 near-tangent groups: `near-crossed-external`, `near-crossed-internal`, `near-crossed-steinmetz`, `near-sphere-box`), `hard`
(S2's sweep, gap at most 1e-13, 1162 items) and `edge` (S2's sweep, nominal gap within 1e-8 relative of `eps`, 592 items).

| Batch | Contents |
|---|---|
| `easy` | Only `easy` |
| `corpus` | Only `corpus`, band 1e-7 |
| `corpus-b0` | Only `corpus`, band zero |
| `hard` | Only `hard`, band 1e-7 |
| `hard-b0` | Only `hard`, band zero: exact tangency questions |
| `mixed` | `easy`, with a run of 32 `edge` items in every block of 1024, at random offsets |
| `mixed-25` | The same, a run of 32 in every 128 |
| `clustered-10` | All `edge` items contiguous at the start, 10% of the batch |
| `edge` | Only `edge` |

The band for `-b0` batches is exactly zero: `c0`'s question asked with the interval filter.

**Schedules.** Lanes inside each task, width 4 unless stated, on `spikes/p11`'s `Pool` (`spikes/p11/src/pool.rs`), the persistent
pool of std primitives that P11 recommends, with the task being a chunk of items claimed in index order. `static` is one
contiguous chunk per thread. `chunk-g` is a fixed chunk of `g` items. `auto` is `n / (threads * 8)` items, the pool's default.
`two-phase-g1-g2` runs a lane pass over chunks of `g1` items that defers each undecided decision, and then one further map over
the deferred exact decisions at grain `g2`. `serial` is one chunk on the calling thread, the plain loop, on a spawned thread
because of the allocator effect P11 measured. Speed-up is against `serial` at the same batch and lane width.

**Imbalance.** Measured per run: per-chunk time (maximum over mean), per-thread busy time (maximum over mean, over the threads
that ran a chunk), and `efficiency` = total busy time over threads times wall time. A chunk's busy time is the time from the
chunk's start to its end, inside the task, so it includes the lane pass and any inline exact work.

**Counts.** `results/count.txt` holds, per batch and lane width, the share of decisions the filter certifies and how many lane
groups have an undecided lane. `results/grain.txt` is a model, no timing: per item, the filter cost plus the exact cost of
each uncertified decision, chunks claimed in index order by the first free thread. It says what speed-up the imbalance alone
allows at each chunk size, with no scheduling cost. `results/exact-tail.txt` is the spread of the exact stage's cost per decision.

**Comparator.** S2's plain `f64` decisions (`a-local` on the same formulas, no filter and no exact stage), in the same harness, chunk
of 1024 items, six passes per repetition: `results/plain.txt`, from `spikes/p6/run/plain.sh`.

**Machine.** Timing on the shared measuring machine: Intel Core i7-8700K, 6 physical cores (12 logical), governor `powersave`,
single-core turbo above all-core, so speed-ups read a little low, and this is not corrected for. Threads pinned with `taskset -c
0-<n-1>`, hyperthreads unused, one process per thread count. Seven repetitions of each configuration; tables give the median
unless stated, and spreads over 5% are listed. Batch sizes were set so that the plain loop takes at least about 120 ms. Output is
hashed with `occam_math::Digest` after the clock stops. `rustc 1.99.0`, `x86_64-unknown-linux-gnu`, empty `RUSTFLAGS`, release
with `lto = "fat"`, `codegen-units = 1`, no `target-cpu`. The counts and the exact-cost spread ran locally, under the memory cap.

## Results

Tables come from `spikes/p6/summarise.py` over `results/lanes.txt` and `results/sched.txt` (`results/tables.txt` has every row),
produced by `spikes/p6/run/all.sh`, and from `results/count.txt`. All timings are the medians of seven repetitions unless
stated. The full table of speed-up at 1, 2, 4 and 6 threads for every batch and schedule is in `results/tables.txt`.

### Determinism

Results are identical at every thread count and lane width. Across the 234 timed rows (one digest per batch for every
combination of `W` = 1, 2, 4, 8 and thread count 1, 2, 4, 6, and every schedule) there is exactly one digest per batch. The
counting pass (`results/count.txt`) agrees across widths on all nine batches, and `lanes_answer_exactly_as_scalar` compares
lanes with the scalar path answer by answer on all 4170 of S2's sweep instances. Lane grouping and chunk boundaries cannot
reach the output: answers are written back by item index, and the exact stage answers one decision at a time.

### What the filter certifies

Counted locally, 131,072 decisions per batch (`results/count.txt`). `groups with an undecided lane` is the share of full lane
groups in which at least one lane needs the exact path; the rest of that group's lanes keep their certified answers.

| Batch | Band | Certified | Reach the exact path | Groups with an undecided lane, W=4 / W=8 |
|---|---|---:|---:|---|
| `easy` | 1e-7 | 100.00% | 0.00% | 0% / 0% |
| `corpus` (the near-tangent groups) | 1e-7 | 100.00% | 0.00% | 0% / 0% |
| `corpus-b0` | 0 | 100.00% | 0.00% | 0% / 0% |
| `hard` (gap at most 1e-13) | 1e-7 | 100.00% | 0.00% | 0% / 0% |
| `hard-b0` (the same inputs, exact sign) | 0 | 13.67% | 86.33% | 100% / 100% |
| `mixed` (3% band-edge items in runs of 32) | 1e-7 | 97.19% | 2.81% | 4.3% / 5.8% |
| `mixed-25` | 1e-7 | 77.69% | 22.31% | 34.1% / 45.7% |
| `clustered-10` | 1e-7 | 91.04% | 8.96% | 10.0% / 10.0% |
| `edge` | 1e-7 | 10.59% | 89.41% | 100% / 100% |

**The premise is wrong for the corpus, and that is the result.** The near-tangent corpus groups are not hard for this filter.
All 72 groups, at `eps` = 1e-7 and even at band zero, are certified: every one of the 131,072 decisions. Their nominal gaps run
from 1.2e-9 to 0.45 mm, median 3.2e-5 (`results/count.txt`). "Near tangent" is hard only for a decision whose quantity is within
the interval's resolution of the band edge, and that resolution is near 1e-15 of the size of the terms of the polynomial, so
about 1e-15 of the radius in the quantity. A band of 1e-7 mm moves the question away from the tangency: a gap of 1e-9 or 1e-13 is
decided at once as "inside the band". The same `hard` inputs at band zero, the exact sign of the tangency, are hard (`hard-b0`,
86% exact; S2's `c0` gave 89% with its filter on the same set). What sends work to the exact path at the model's band is
`|q| = eps` within rounding (`edge`, 89%). No corpus group is there, which agrees with S2's finding that the corpus does not reach
the band edge.

| `hard` | 0% | 46.0 [46.2] | 39.6 [39.8] | 32.4 [32.6] | 33.0 [33.1] | 1.42 |
| `mixed` | 2.8% | 136.9 [137.7] | 131.4 [132.5] | 121.7 [123.3] | 124.4 [124.6] | 1.12 |
| `clustered-10` | 8.9% | 336.1 [336.9] | 330.0 [331.4] | 321.0 [322.7] | 321.9 [324.3] | 1.05 |
| `mixed-25` | 22% | 775.8 [782.3] | 775.1 [778.1] | 768.2 [772.5] | 767.3 [772.9] | 1.01 |
| `hard-b0` | 86% | 2595 [2602] | 2579 [2602] | 2572 [2594] | 2573 [2591] | 1.01 |

- **A filtered decision costs 44 to 56 ns scalar and 30 to 38 ns at four lanes** on the certified batches: lanes gain 1.5 at W=4,
  and W=8 gains nothing more (31 ns against 30). This is SSE2 at `base`, so W=8 is two 128-bit halves; the wider-lane results of
  P4 through `dispatch!` are not tried here. The S2 cost of 52 to 58 ns per decision is the same ballpark as `W=1`, 44 to 56 ns
  here (a slightly different harness and `I<f64>` per decision).
- **The exact path costs about 3.3 µs per decision**, from the batches that use it: `(121.7 - 0.972 * 29.8) / 0.0279 = 3.29 µs`
  on `mixed`, `(2935.2 - 29.8) / 0.894 = 3.25 µs` on `edge`, 2.95 µs on `hard-b0`. That is about 110 times the four-lane
  filter. It gets no lane gain, and individual exact decisions vary widely: 0.9 to 22 µs on a local reading
  (`results/exact-tail.txt`, median 5.6 µs on a slower, loaded machine), a ratio of 25 between the cheapest and dearest.
- **Lanes speed up only what the filter certifies, so the fallback sets the gain.** A run with 2.8% of its decisions on the exact
  path spends about 75% of its time there, and four lanes gain 1.12 instead of 1.48. Below about 1% exact the filter dominates; at 1% the two are equal,
  and above that the cost is the exact share times 3.3 µs. The model `t(W) = t_f(W) + p t_e`, with `t_f(4)` = 30 and `t_e` = 3250 ns,
  predicts `mixed`, `mixed-25` and `clustered-10` to within 2% (`edge` supplied `t_e`). `hard-b0` reads 10% cheaper than it, its
  exact decisions being cheaper.

### Where the work piles up

Thread busy time is the measure of a pile-up: if one thread does the work of the others, its busy time is above the mean, and if
others wait, `efficiency` falls. At six threads (`results/sched.txt`):

| Batch | static: thread busy max/mean | static efficiency | chunk-256: max/mean | chunk-1024: max/mean | chunk-1024 task max/mean |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `easy` | 1.09 | 0.91 | 1.00 | 1.00 | 20.8 (tasks of 60 µs; noise) |
| `corpus` | 1.02 | 0.97 | 1.00 | 1.00 | 7.9 |
| `hard-b0` | 1.04 | 0.95 | 1.02 | 1.12 | 1.1 |
| `mixed` | 1.07 | 0.87 | 1.00 | 1.01 | 2.5 |
| `mixed-25` | 1.03 | 0.97 | 1.01 | 1.02 | 1.4 |
| `clustered-10` | **5.52** | **0.18** | 1.00 | 1.03 | 9.8 |
| `edge` | 1.02 | 0.98 | 1.02 | 1.09 | 1.3 |

Only one case piles up: all the hard work contiguous (`clustered-10`) under a static split. One thread gets the hard tenth, the
others finish early, and at six threads the run reaches 1.07× instead of 5.5×. Uniformly easy or uniformly hard batches are
balanced by any schedule that has enough tasks. Dispersed hard work (`mixed`, `mixed-25`) is balanced too: the longest task of
1024 items is at most 2.5 times the mean (1.4 in `mixed-25`), because any such chunk holds about the same number of runs of 32
hard items.

**Chunk size against the imbalance.** The counting model in `results/grain.txt` predicts the speed-up imbalance alone allows, with
`t_f` = 29.8 and `t_e` = 3250 ns, at the same batch sizes. At six threads:

The model is within 5 to 7% of the measured speed-up (the gap is scheduling cost and the all-core clock) and gives the shape: a

4096, 32 of 1024, 128 of 256: too few tasks per thread lose the balance as they would for any uneven work.

of the plain loop, `chunk-1024` 0.98 to 1.04).

### Separating easy and hard work

Two-phase runs the lane pass over chunks of `g1` items and defers undecided decisions, then runs one map over the deferred exact
work at grain `g2`. It keeps the lane tasks uniform and takes the hard work out of them.

| Batch | chunk-1024 T6 | chunk-256 T6 | two-phase g1=4096, g2=8 | two-phase g1=1024, g2=1 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `easy` | 5.46 | 5.23 | 5.43 | 5.44 |
| `mixed` | 5.56 | 5.42 | 5.32 | 4.66 |
| `mixed-25` | 5.53 | 5.40 | 5.43 | 4.34 |
| `clustered-10` | 5.41 | 5.59 | 5.36 | 4.26 |
| `hard-b0` | 5.23 | 5.50 | 5.17 | 4.17 |
| `edge` | 5.09 | 5.45 | 5.32 | 4.30 |

Separating does not pay on any of these batches. Against the better of chunk 256 and 1024, two-phase at `g2` = 8 is 0.5 to 6%
slower, and at `g2` = 1, one exact decision per task of about 3 µs, 16 to 24% slower: the same effect as chunk-16, each task paying for
its claim and its result `Vec` (at two threads it reaches only 1.5 to 1.65 where one pass reaches 1.9). Its one advantage: the
first-phase grain no longer affects the balance, so a badly chosen `g1` does not hurt (at `g1` = 4096 it reaches 5.36× on
`clustered-10`, where one pass at chunk 4096 reaches 4.78×). Fixing the chunk size is simpler and reaches the same speed.

Six of the 234 rows have a median more than 5% above their minimum: `hard-b0` two-phase-1024-1 at 2 threads (5.3%), `easy` static
at 4 (5.1%), `mixed-25` static at 4 (6.3%), `mixed` static at 6 (7.8%), `mixed` auto at 6 (5.1%), and `edge` auto at 6 (7.3%).

## Answer

**The robust path in a parallel run is the filter, with a cost that does not grow with the threads, and on the corpus's near-tangent
groups the exact path is not reached at all.** In numbers:

- Certified decisions cost 30 to 38 ns at four lanes (44 to 56 ns scalar) and scale 5.5 to 5.7 times at six cores, the same as or a
  little better than the plain `f64` loop (5.3). A robust decision at six threads is 5.5 to 7 ns of wall time, 3.5 to 4.5 times plain
  `f64` at six threads (1.5 ns); the ratio is about the same as at one thread (3.7 to 4.8). Lanes and threads multiply as P4 said: four lanes (1.48)
  times six threads (5.5) is 8.2 over one scalar thread, measured 8.2.
- **The near-tangent corpus does not reach the exact path at all**: 100% of its decisions certified, at the band and at zero. A
  near-tangent batch costs the filter's price, no more. The expected pile-up on the corpus's near-tangent groups does not exist.
- **The exact path costs 3.3 µs per decision**, 110 times the four-lane filter, gains nothing from lanes, and scales normally (5.1 to
  5.7 times at six cores when the batch is uniform). So each 1% of decisions that fall through adds 33 ns per decision on one thread,
  a little over 6 ns at six. A run with 2.8% of its decisions at the band edge spends about 75% of its time on them, and four lanes
  gain 1.12 instead of 1.48. The rate at which real models reach the band edge is what sets the cost, and it is not known yet.

**Should easy and hard work be separated? No.** One pass, filter in lanes and exact inline, with dynamically claimed chunks, is
balanced on every batch measured. A second, deferred pass for exact work was 0.5 to 6% slower at its best grain and 16 to 24% slower
at a grain of one exact decision per task. Its only benefit, insensitivity to the first-phase grain, is bought more cheaply by using
the right chunk size.

**The imbalance is real in one case and small grain fixes it.** All of the hard work contiguous under a static split: 1.07 times
instead of 5.5 at six threads, one thread with 5.5 times the mean busy time. Uniformly easy, uniformly hard and dispersed hard work all
balance. Dynamic claiming of chunks of 256 or 1024 items reaches 5.1 to 5.7 times, whatever the arrangement of the hard items.

**The grain the scheduler needs: tasks of about 15 to 60 µs of filter work, 256 to 1024 items, claimed dynamically, with about 20 to 40
tasks per thread.** Chunk 1024 reaches at least 5.09× at six threads on every batch (median 5.53×), and chunk 256 at least 5.23×. Below that
the claiming wins: 16 items, about 1 µs a task, halves the speed-up on certified work (2.7×); 64 items lose 14 to 19% there. Above that the balance goes: 4096
items reach 3.9× on the small hard batches (8 tasks) and 4.8× on the clustered one. The pool's `n / (threads * 8)` is good, except on
clustered hard work (4.82×), where its 48 tasks are too few. Hard chunks take up to ten times as long as the mean (9.8× on
`clustered-10` at 1024 items), and the exact decisions themselves vary by 25 times: this is what the dynamic claim absorbs.

Results are identical at every thread count and lane width (234 timed rows, one digest per batch, in every combination measured).

## What this does not show

- **How often a real boolean reaches the band edge.** The corpus's 72 near-tangent groups never do, at `eps` = 1e-7 or at zero;
  `edge` and `hard-b0` are the slow path by construction. The rate in real models is the number that matters, and it is not
  measured here. `proving-ground.s1` is the place to count it.
- **Only S2's decisions.** D1 and D4 over spheres, planes and cylinders, written as low-degree polynomials. No NURBS, no marching,
  no boolean, no computed values with a certified radius in a parallel run (S2 measured those at 91 ns a step on one thread).
- **The exact path is S2's, with allocating expansions.** It costs microseconds and it allocates on every operation. An arena or a
  thread-local scratch buffer was not tried, so what the exact path can cost and scale to is not settled.
- **Geometry from a small table.** Batches repeat a pool of 72 to 1162 distinct items, so cache behaviour is friendlier than walking
  a real model. The gather into lanes is a plain copy of `Item` values.
- **SSE2 only.** `Lanes<W>` in a `base` build, no `target-cpu`, no `fearless_simd`, so W=8 reaches at most 128-bit vectors. The
  AVX2 reach that `proving-ground.p4` measured through `dispatch!` is not tried here.
- **One machine**, six physical cores, Coffee Lake, `powersave`, shared with other investigations (lock-separated, with a load average
  of 1.4 to 2.8 around the runs). Lane gains and speed-ups are from this machine alone. The one-thread baseline was pinned to CPU 0,
  and some speed-ups above 5.49 suggest it may read slow; I did not investigate. Six rows have spreads above 5% (listed above).
- **`thread_busy_max_over_mean` counts only the threads that ran at least one chunk.** A schedule that leaves some threads idle
  (a single large chunk) reads low on that column; `efficiency` shows those idle threads.
- **The model in `results/grain.txt` is a model**: it assumes the filter and exact costs are constants, and it has no scheduling cost.
  It is for the shape of the imbalance; the measured speed-ups stand on their own.
- **The `corpus`, `hard` and `easy` batches are 100% certified.** Their digests equal plain `f64`'s (same answers), so they show the
  filter's cost and scaling, not the value of having it. S2 has the value (wrong answers against nominal, composition).
- **The exact-path spread (`results/exact-tail.txt`) is a local reading** on a loaded machine: only the ratios are used, not its 5.6 µs
  median, which is higher than the 3.3 µs the measuring machine's batches give.
- **Only chunk sizes 16, 64, 256, 1024 and 4096 were run**, 512 not, and two-phase only at `g1` of 1024 and 4096.
- **A batch of 2 million items is big.** Whether the pool's costs hold on small batches (a few hundred items, an interactive edit) is
  not covered here, except the small hard batches.

## What it decides

For `proving-ground.design`, if the user accepts S2's tolerance model:

- **The cost figure for the kernel's robust predicates is the filter in lanes**: 30 to 38 ns per decision on x86-64 at four lanes
  in `base`, 5.5 to 6.8 ns of wall time at six cores, about 4 times plain `f64` at any thread count. The S2 ratio of 8 was a scalar
  figure; lanes halve it.
- **A batch of robust decisions runs in one pass**: the filter in `Lanes<4>` inside a task, grouped by family inside the task, exact
  fallback per decision inline, on chunks of 256 to 1024 items claimed dynamically, with one task for at least 15 µs of filter
  work and enough tasks for about 20 to 40 per thread. Static partitioning is ruled out for any batch that can contain hard work. Fallback is
  not separated into its own pass or scheduled at finer grain than the filter.
- **Lane width for predicates is 4 under `base`** (`Lanes<8>` buys nothing without AVX2; W=2 gains 1.13).
- **The fallback rate is the number to record and gate on**: report the share of decisions reaching the exact path in the kernel's
  stats, because every 1% adds 33 ns per decision on one thread, and at 1% the exact work already equals the filter work.
  `proving-ground.s1`'s boolean and the census's real models should count it.
- **Expansions need no special treatment for parallelism yet**: they allocate on every operation and still scale 5.1 to 5.7 times. A
  scratch arena remains a possible speed-up of the exact path itself, not a scaling need.

## Open questions for the user

None that need a decision now. The measure that remains is the rate at which real models reach the band edge, which only a real
boolean can give (`proving-ground.s1`), and with it whether the 3.3 µs of the exact path is worth an arena.
