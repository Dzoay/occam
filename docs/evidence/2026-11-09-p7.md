# P7: does another representation help the boolean?

Plan step `proving-ground.p7` ([the milestone](../plan/proving-ground.md)). Throwaway code in `spikes/p7/`, on branch `spike-p7`, built on S1's prototype by path. Raw rows and the commands are in `spikes/p7/results/`. S1's limits (its finding, `2026-11-09-s1.md`) carry over to everything measured here.

## Question

Does a mesh-first topology seed (b), or implicit fields as a broad-phase cull (c), earn a place in the boolean design, or does pure B-rep (a, S1 as it stands) stand?

## What was measured

**Baseline (a).** S1's boolean, run on the same operands, with its exact section curves as the reference.

**Candidate (b), on 212 near-tangent operand pairs,** one per group: the `intersection` case of the 18 groups of each near-tangent corpus family (`near-crossed-external`, `-internal`, `-steinmetz`, `near-sphere-box`), 60 `nurbs-near-peak` blocks (seeds 21 and 9901) and 80 near-tangent crossed-random pairs (seeds 5 and 7701).

Each surface is meshed whole at resolution `n`: cylinders `2n` columns by `max(n/4, 2)` rows, spheres `n` by `2n` latitude-longitude, the NURBS top `n` by `n`, rectangular plane faces as two triangles. The meshes are intersected with orientation predicates evaluated in a canonical point order, so the same four points always give the same sign and the crossing events close up combinatorially. (Double precision, not exact arithmetic; the canonical-order idea follows Edelsbrunner and Mücke, 1990, not re-read.) Crossing events are linked into components, and a seed is the list of components per surface pair, each closed or open.

*Reference:* the connected components of the section edges in S1's result, per surface pair. *In scope:* cylinder-cylinder, sphere-plane, NURBS-plane. Plane-plane pairs, and so the block side faces, are out of scope.

*Reporting rule:* a seed is **reported** when a triangle pair lies within the two meshes' sag but is not next to any intersected triangle, meaning a contact the mesh cannot resolve. Otherwise it commits, and is **correct** or **wrong** against the reference.

Resolutions 16, 32, 64 and 128. A run that added 256 and 512 hit a 280 s timeout, so those were not measured. **Control:** the same run with the cylinder columns rotated by 0.37 of a cell (16, 64, 128), and at `n` = 128 also by 0.5 and 0.13 of a cell. The control was added after the first run, because the first run's counts did not change with `n`. Those two extra runs hit a 60 s timeout after 124 and 116 of the 212 pairs, so only the 72 corpus groups are complete for them.

**Candidate (c), culling only.** S1's broad phase (padded boxes) against an added test: 8 by 8 samples of one face against the other surface's signed distance, with a Lipschitz bound on each cell (the bound as in Hart's sphere tracing, 1996, not re-read; the first-derivative maximum is sampled and doubled, so it is not a proof). Counted on 320 operand pairs: the 212 near-tangent pairs above plus 108 other corpus cases (`steinmetz`, `sphere-cap`, `sphere-lens`, `coaxial-stepped`, `box-corner`, `plate-hole*`), where common, cut and fuse of the same operands count separately. Every culled pair was re-checked at 49 by 49 points. **The re-check was changed after it flagged 14 pairs:** the first version required both faces to stay off the other's surface, but a pair is culled by one direction only, and a face may straddle the other's surface beyond the other face. The final version checks only the direction that culled, and flags none. The change is argued, not independently tested.

**Speed-up at 1, 2, 4 and 6 threads: not measured.** No parallel code was written and the measuring machine was not used. All counts ran locally under the 3 GB cap. The `micros` column in `seed.tsv` is a laptop timing and is not a measurement.

**The topology errors are confident.** All 34 at `n` = 128 under the shift have one closed loop where S1's section has two (`LL` becoming `L`), and none of them was reported: the reporting rule does not catch them. Whether the two loops merged or one was lost was not distinguished. With columns as built the same cases come out right; why was not isolated.

**"S1 empty, seed not" looks like grid alignment on the cylinders.** In these 24 to 26 cases S1's result has no section. In the four `near-crossed-external` corpus cases S1's volume is 0, and in the two whose parameters were checked (0008 and 0009) the overlap is 4e-8 and 1.2e-9 mm, below the 1e-7 mm model tolerance. Under the shift all the crossed cases of this kind disappear, which fits a loop appearing only when a mesh column lands on the tangent point, but that cause was not tested directly, and the depths of the crossed-random cases were not checked. The 8 `nurbs-near-peak` cases of this kind are not evidence either way: the shift does not touch the NURBS mesh.

**S1's three failures.** On the three `nurbs-near-peak` blocks S1 reports, the seed finds at least one component on the NURBS-plane pair at `n` of 64 and above. At 16 and 32 it finds nothing on one block, which the rule reports. This is a weak result: S1 had the piece on the NURBS as well, and its failure in the one block diagnosed was losing the matching pieces on the side faces. The side faces are out of the seed's scope, so **the seed does not touch what S1 failed on.**

**Resolution needed.** `near-sphere-box` misses the section in 11 of 18 groups even at `n` = 128, all reported. (The shift applies to cylinders only, so these rows do not change under it.) By the chord sag `r(1 - cos(pi/N))`, seeing a depth `d` needs about `N = pi sqrt(r / 2d)`: about 2,200 columns for `r` = 10 mm and `d` = 1e-5 mm, and about 22,000 for `d` = 1e-7 mm. That is an estimate from the formula, not a run. Going by the same sag, 512 columns gives a sag of about 2e-4 mm at `r` = 10 mm, and S1's overlaps go down to 1e-9 mm.

## Answer

**Pure B-rep stands. Neither candidate earns a place in the boolean design on this evidence.**

The mesh seed **moves the failure to the mesh** rather than removing it. On the cases where S1 is already right it adds nothing, and at `n` = 128 an ordinary shift of the mesh turns 34 of them into confident wrong topology. The 162 correct with columns as built fall to 101 when the columns move by 0.37 of a cell, and every shift tried gives wrong topology on 8 to 10 of the 18 corpus internal groups, against none as built. So a good part of the agreement near tangency depended on where the grid happened to fall. The seed did not reach S1's own failures. The reporting rule caught the missed sections but none of the 34 miscounted loops, so a design could not trust a seed that comes back unreported.

Implicit culling is safe here and removes 12% of pairs and 7% of edge tests, but none on NURBS, and it would save only part of the pair phase, which is 11 to 26% of S1's boolean time on the analytic families; face split, at 60 to 72%, is untouched. On NURBS the pair phase is 52% but the test removed nothing. How much time it would save was not measured. It belongs, if anywhere, with the work-avoidance question in `proving-ground.p8`, not in the boolean's design.

## What this does not show

- That a mesh seed is useless everywhere. Only near-tangent groups were tested, and those are where a seed is weakest. Transversal cases, where a seed might be cheap and right, were not measured, and neither was a seed used as a hint instead of as the topology.
- That better predicates or a conforming, curvature-adaptive mesh would not do better. The mesh is uniform in parameter space, and no trim-conforming or adaptive tessellation was tried.
- Anything above `n` = 128: 256 and 512 were not completed.
- That the shift is representative. Three phases were tried, on the cylinders only, and only 0.37 covers all 212 pairs. It does not touch the sphere or NURBS meshes.
- The side faces of the NURBS blocks, disc-capped plane pairs and curved-trim planes, all out of scope.
- That S1's sections are the truth at the tolerance scale. S1 drops contacts thinner than the tolerance, so "S1 empty, seed not" is a disagreement about a convention as much as a mistake, and was verified only in two cases. S1's near-tangent reference is itself checked only as its finding says.
- Any speed: neither the time of a seed nor the speed-up at 1, 2, 4 and 6 threads was measured.
- That culling is safe in general: the bound is a sampled estimate and the re-check is a finite sample. Its benefit on large models is unknown: these are small models with few faces, and culling may matter more with many faces per pair.

## What it decides

Nothing is decided here. This is evidence for `proving-ground.design`:

- Keep the boolean's section computation on the exact surfaces; do not put a mesh seed in the path that decides topology.
- If a mesh ever feeds a boolean, it must not be trusted when it comes back unreported.
- Treat implicit culling as an optional optimisation for `proving-ground.p8` to weigh, not as part of the representation.
