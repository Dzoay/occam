# `proving-ground.s4`: seams across families

Evidence for `proving-ground.design`. Code, raw results and the commands that produced them are in `spikes/s4/` on the
`spike-s4` branch. The sources that were measured are commit `e853d76`.

## Question

Can one `Surface` and `Curve` interface carry analytic, NURBS and mesh-backed bodies, with closed enums inside a family,
open traits across families and no downcasting (L21, L26)? What can it not express, and what does each dispatch form
cost?

## What was measured

**The code.** Five analytic surfaces (plane, cylinder, cone, sphere, torus) behind one closed enum; rational NURBS
curves and surfaces of arbitrary degree (*The NURBS Book*, 2nd ed., A2.1 to A2.3, A3.2 and A3.6, A4.2 and A4.4,
equations 4.8 and 4.20); a triangle mesh presented through the same interface, each vertex carrying a `(u, v)` (closest
point from Ericson, *Real-Time Collision Detection*, section 5.1.5). To try to break the seams I added two more families
and a wrapper: the extrusion of any curve; the offset of any surface (the normal's derivative from do Carmo,
*Differential Geometry of Curves and Surfaces*, section 3.2); and `Placed<S, A, B>`, a surface carried rigidly from one
frame to another.

**The checks.** 40 tests pass at `e853d76`. The 17 conformance tests include one suite of 8 that runs over the same 20
surfaces across all the families above: agreement between orders, batches against single calls, finite differences,
closest points, bounds, seams, degenerate lines and normals. Finite differences stop at the first derivative on the C0
mesh. The other 9 check named cases, closed forms and fingerprints. A further 13 tests cover choosing an intersection
method, 3 the batching store, 3 are checks on the source text (the cross-family layer names no family except the
analytic forms, and nothing recovers a concrete type) and 3 are compile-fail doctests. One test checks bit-identical
results over 15 surfaces at lane widths 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 and 8. The alternatives' real compiler errors are in
`results/compile-alternatives.txt`.

**The timings.** Intel Core i7-8700K, 6 physical cores, governor `powersave`, `rustc 1.99.0 (b940084d7 2026-09-28)`,
`x86_64-unknown-linux-gnu`, LTO fat, one codegen unit, pinned to one physical core. Two builds: no `RUSTFLAGS`, which is
the kernel's one build, and `-C target-cpu=x86-64-v3`. Nine repetitions of each configuration, reported as the median.
Single thread only: threads are not part of this question. Commands: `results/sweep.sh` and `results/sweep-resume.sh`;
tables from `results/tables.py`, output in `results/tables.txt`.

**Provenance of the runs.** Results come from three grouped remote commands. `raw-a.tsv` and `raw-b.tsv` are complete.
`raw-c.tsv` holds complete `closest`, `mesh_paths` and `pairs` blocks and then stops at 52 of the 100 v3 `widths` rows,
where the machine controlling the run crashed; the v3 `degree` run had not started. `raw-c-v3.tsv` re-runs only those
two. `raw-c.tsv` is kept as written, and the cut-off rows are not used. Nothing under `src/` has changed since
`e853d76`.

**Where the protocol was not met.**

- 36 of the 370 timed rows ran for less than 100 ms per repetition (43.7 to 99.8 ms). Calibration sizes each
  configuration against one form, and the faster forms in that configuration come in under the target. They were not
  re-run. Their largest spread is 6.6%.
- Two rows have a median more than 5% above the minimum: `dispatch` plane generic at order 0 (9.1%, 3.134 and 3.418 ns)
  and `widths-v3` plane `f64` at order 0 (6.6%, 2.770 and 2.953 ns). Both are the first row of their experiment, at
  about 3 ns per point. I did not find out why. The same plane code showed at most 0.9% in two other runs, so I read
  them as noise in a very short measurement, and no conclusion below depends on those two numbers.
- Each block calibrates its own point count, so digests are comparable only within a block. Digests of the generic build
  and the v3 build were never comparable: none of their 100 `widths` rows share a count.

## Results

### Per-call dispatch

Median ns per evaluation, baseline build. `generic` is monomorphised on the concrete surface; `enum` is generic over the
family's enum, so it includes the match; `dyn` is a virtual call, on the concrete surface. Digests agree across forms
for all 39 surface and order combinations. The plane's 3.4 ns at order 0 is the noisy row described above, and its
minimum is 3.1 ns.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| plane | 0 | 3.4 | 4.4 | 3.5 | 4.7 |
| plane | 2 | 5.0 | 16.6 | 13.8 | 17.0 |
| cylinder | 0 | 15.6 | 17.5 | 16.5 | 17.6 |
| cylinder | 2 | 26.0 | 31.0 | 31.0 | 31.1 |
| cone | 0 | 15.4 | 17.8 | 16.6 | 17.7 |
| sphere | 0 | 24.4 | 26.3 | 25.6 | 26.7 |
| sphere | 2 | 39.0 | 42.3 | 43.1 | 42.6 |
| torus | 0 | 30.2 | 31.2 | 31.2 | 31.0 |
| torus | 2 | 45.3 | 48.5 | 50.1 | 48.6 |
| bicubic | 0 | 44.3 | | 45.0 | |
| bicubic | 2 | 139 | | 141 | |
| rational bicubic | 0 | 51.1 | | 51.5 | |

| rational degree 7 by 7 (heap) | 0 | 806 | | 768 | |

- A virtual call costs 0.9 to 1.6 ns at orders 0 and 1 on the curved analytic surfaces (3 to 8%), and 0 to 3% on most
  NURBS and mesh rows. The rational sphere reaches 8% at order 0. The degree 7 by 7 heap path is 3 to 5% *faster*
  through `dyn` at every order. I read that as code layout, not as a property of `dyn`, and did not investigate it.
- At order 2 the `dyn` premium is 4 to 7 ns on the curved surfaces (11 to 23%). On the plane it is 8.9 ns, or 2.8 times,
  and the plane's second derivatives are all zero. Probably the generic form folds those constants away and the virtual
  call has to build and return a 144-byte `Jet2`. I did not measure that separately.
- An enum match costs about what a virtual call costs: within about 1.5 ns at orders 0 and 1. Adding `dyn` to an enum
  adds at most 0.4 ns at order 0.
- Asking for only what is needed pays (L27): 44, 85 and 139 ns for a bicubic at orders 0, 1 and 2.

**The cone: where the call site matters more than the dispatch.** The first run computed `sin` and `cos` of the
half-angle inside each `eval`. A generic caller gets that hoisted out of its loop, and a `dyn` or enum caller does not,
so `dyn` looked 56% slower than generic at order 0. Computing it once at construction took `dyn` from 24.1 to 16.6 ns
and the enum from 24.2 to 17.8 ns, and generic stayed at 15.4 ns. The earlier run is kept as
`results/dispatch-before-cone-cache.tsv`.

### One `dyn` call per slice

Median ns per point; `W=4` lanes inside the call; baseline build.

| surface | per call | slice 1 | slice 4 | slice 16 | slice 64 | slice 4096 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| cylinder | 16.6 | 20.5 | 15.4 | 14.6 | 14.5 | 14.5 |
| sphere | 25.6 | 29.1 | 25.6 | 25.4 | 24.6 | 24.5 |
| bicubic | 45.0 | 50.0 | 47.8 | 49.0 | 48.8 | 49.5 |
| mesh, 8192 triangles | 94.7 | 103 | 114 | 115 | 114 | 115 |

A slice of one costs 9 to 23% more than a plain call. Slicing helps the cylinder from 4 points up, by 7 to 13%. It
barely helps the sphere, and it makes the bicubic 6 to 10% and the mesh about 20% slower.

### Lane widths

`W=4` per point against scalar, `f64 / W=4`, so above 1 means lanes win. The lane forms include the seam's cost of
filling lanes from a slice and reading them back.

| surface | order | baseline | v3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| plane | 1 | 0.52x | 0.54x |
| cylinder | 0 | 1.10x | 1.06x |
| cylinder | 2 | 0.58x | 0.72x |
| sphere | 2 | 0.65x | 0.61x |
| bicubic | 0 | 0.88x | 0.98x |
| bicubic | 2 | 0.91x | 1.07x |
| rational bicubic | 2 | 0.78x | 1.02x |
| degree 5 by 4 (heap) | 0 | 2.56x | 2.54x |
| mesh, 8192 triangles | 0 | 0.80x | 0.76x |

Over all 19 rows other than the heap one, the median is **0.90x** on the baseline build and **1.01x** on v3, ranging
from 0.52x to 1.17x. `W=8` is worse (median 0.75x and 0.87x). **Lanes do not buy speed on this seam as `occam-math`
stands.** The plan should know this: L28 commits lanes to composing with threads, and here lanes bring no speed to
compose. It does agree with L27, which puts avoided work ahead of SIMD. The likely reason is in
`spikes/s4/src/lanes_ext.rs`: `Real` is arithmetic, `abs` and `sqrt` only, so `sin_cos`, knot spans and mesh point
location run one lane at a time inside the "lane" call.

The one large win is not SIMD. Above degree 3 a NURBS evaluation allocates on every call, and a lane call shares one
allocation across four points. A bicubic forced onto the heap path shows the size of that: 380 ns scalar and 141 ns at
`W=4`, against 45 and 47 ns on the stack. The heap path costs 8.4 times the stack path as scalar, and the v3 build gives
the same picture (380 and 139, against 45 and 45).

**Bit-identical across widths: yes.** Digests agree at `f64` and `W=1, 2, 4, 8` on all 20 surface and order combinations
in both builds.

### Mixed surfaces in one loop

Median ns per evaluation; baseline build.

| workload | enum | `dyn` | generic | store columns |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5000 analytic surfaces of 5 kinds, shuffled | 24.7 | 27.2 | | 63.8 |
| the same, sorted by kind | 21.2 | 22.2 | | |
| 512 NURBS surfaces of 4 degree classes, shuffled | | 136.9 | 133.2 | 127.0 |

Sorting by kind (L29) is worth 14 to 18%. The store, which groups by kind and degree and runs lanes across instances,
returns identical digests to the per-call forms, and is 7% better than `dyn` on NURBS but **2.6 times worse** than the
enum on analytic surfaces. It re-sorts 5000 queries and allocates on every pass and gathers frames through closures. I
did not break that cost down, so this measures this implementation, not batching. Sorted per-call is the cheap version
of L29 that these numbers support.

The mesh path shows the same thing as the slices: 84.3 ns as `f64`, 112.2 ns at `W=4`, 98.9 ns through a store column.

### Closest point

All through `dyn Surface`, no seed, baseline build: plane 15.9 ns, cylinder 55.3 ns, sphere 74.9 ns, torus 88.7 ns,
bicubic by Newton 30.4 microseconds, rational sphere 21.1 microseconds, mesh by scan 60.3 microseconds. Dispatch is not
visible at these sizes. The choice of method is, and so is whether a seed exists.

### Choosing a method for a pair

The 66 unordered pairs of an 11-surface zoo were claimed by the closed forms 11 times, a family's own method twice, the
plane-against-triangles method twice and the general method 51 times. The pairs tested give the same method and curves
in either order. A NURBS patch that is really a plane takes the closed form, a surface placed through a rigid map keeps
its analytic form, and analytic on both sides does not imply a closed form: a cone and a cylinder go to the general
method. The curve counts from the deliberately simple general method are not evidence of its quality.

The alternatives fail to compile when a family is added, in `results/compile-alternatives.txt`: the enum over families
(`E0004`, non-exhaustive match over pairs), double dispatch (`E0046`, one more method on every implementor) and a
generic fallback beside specific pairs (`E0119`).

## Answer

**Yes, with two layers and a short list of questions a surface can answer.** Every family passed the same suite. No
`Any`, no downcast, and adding the extrusion and the offset edited no list or match over families. Dispatch cost was
never what decided where the seam sits: a virtual call is 1 to 2 ns on an evaluation of 15 to 90 ns.

This type sketch is `spikes/s4/tests/sketch.rs` between its markers, and `cargo test --test sketch` compiles and runs
it.

```rust
use std::marker::PhantomData;

use occam_math::{Lanes, Real};
use occam_report::Report;
use occam_units::{Frame, Length, Point, Tolerance, UvPoint};

/// Not a `Vector`, which is three lengths: a derivative is length per parameter, and the parameter may be an angle.
#[derive(Clone, Copy, Debug, PartialEq)]
pub struct Tangent<F: Frame, R: Real = f64>(pub [R; 3], pub PhantomData<F>);

/// A dimensionless unit axis.
#[derive(Clone, Copy, Debug, PartialEq)]
pub struct Direction<F: Frame, R: Real = f64>(pub [R; 3], pub PhantomData<F>);

/// How a parameter's ends meet.
#[derive(Clone, Copy, Debug, PartialEq, Eq)]
pub enum Wrap {
    Open,
    Closed,
    Periodic,
}

#[derive(Clone, Copy, Debug, PartialEq)]
pub struct ParamRange {
    pub lo: f64,
    pub hi: f64,
    pub wrap: Wrap,
}

#[derive(Clone, Copy, Debug, PartialEq)]
pub struct Domain {
    pub u: ParamRange,
    pub v: ParamRange,
}

#[derive(Clone, Copy, Debug, PartialEq, Eq)]
pub enum Param {
    U,
    V,
}

/// A parameter line that collapses to a point. A sphere pole has a normal there and a cone's apex does not.
#[derive(Clone, Copy, Debug, PartialEq)]
pub struct Degeneracy<F: Frame> {
    pub param: Param,
    pub at: f64,
    pub point: Point<F>,
    pub normal: Option<Direction<F>>,
}

/// Which items of a family can share a lane loop (L29).
#[derive(Clone, Copy, Debug, PartialEq, Eq, PartialOrd, Ord)]
pub struct BatchKey {
    pub kind: u32,
    pub degree: (u16, u16),
}

#[derive(Clone, Copy, Debug, PartialEq)]
pub struct Jet1<F: Frame, R: Real = f64> {
    pub p: Point<F, R>,
    pub du: Tangent<F, R>,
    pub dv: Tangent<F, R>,
}

#[derive(Clone, Copy, Debug, PartialEq)]
pub struct Jet2<F: Frame, R: Real = f64> {
    pub p: Point<F, R>,
    pub du: Tangent<F, R>,
    pub dv: Tangent<F, R>,
    pub duu: Tangent<F, R>,
    pub duv: Tangent<F, R>,
    pub dvv: Tangent<F, R>,
}

pub struct Closest<F: Frame> {
    pub uv: UvPoint,
    pub point: Point<F>,
    pub distance: Length,
}

/// The exact forms a surface can present, however it is stored: closed, and the one family the cross-family seam names.
#[derive(Clone, Copy, Debug, PartialEq)]
pub enum Analytic<F: Frame> {
    Plane { origin: Point<F>, normal: Direction<F> },
    Sphere { centre: Point<F>, radius: Length },
}

pub struct Intersection<F: Frame> {
    pub method: &'static str,
    pub points: Vec<Point<F>>,
}

pub trait TriangleView<F: Frame>: Send + Sync {
    fn triangle_count(&self) -> usize;
    fn triangle(&self, index: usize) -> [Point<F>; 3];
}

/// Layer 1, what a family implements: generic over `Real`, so one formula runs as `f64` or in lanes. Not dyn-compatible.
/// `eval_*` is total: an undefined parameter is NaN in that lane, because no lane can carry a `Report`.
pub trait Parametric<F: Frame>: Sized + Send + Sync + 'static {
    fn eval_point<R: Real>(&self, uv: UvPoint<R>) -> Point<F, R>;
    fn eval_first<R: Real>(&self, uv: UvPoint<R>) -> Jet1<F, R>;
    fn eval_second<R: Real>(&self, uv: UvPoint<R>) -> Jet2<F, R>;
    fn domain(&self) -> Domain;
    fn family(&self) -> &'static str;
    fn batch_key(&self) -> BatchKey;
    fn fingerprint(&self) -> u64;
    fn degeneracies(&self) -> Vec<Degeneracy<F>> {
        Vec::new()
    }
    /// # Errors
    /// A report carrying where the solver stopped.
    fn closest(&self, p: Point<F>, seed: Option<UvPoint>, tol: Tolerance) -> Result<Closest<F>, Report>;

    // What the surface can present, asked instead of what it is. Each defaults to "nothing", so a new family edits no list.
    fn analytic(&self, _tol: Tolerance) -> Option<Analytic<F>> {
        None
    }
    fn triangles(&self) -> Option<&dyn TriangleView<F>> {
        None
    }
    fn specialised(&self, _other: &dyn Surface<F>, _tol: Tolerance) -> Option<Result<Intersection<F>, Report>> {
        None
    }
}

/// Layer 2, what everything outside a family calls: dyn-compatible, `f64` per call, lanes inside a slice.
pub trait Surface<F: Frame>: Send + Sync {
    fn point(&self, uv: UvPoint) -> Point<F>;
    fn first(&self, uv: UvPoint) -> Jet1<F>;
    fn second(&self, uv: UvPoint) -> Jet2<F>;
    fn points(&self, uv: &[UvPoint], out: &mut Vec<Point<F>>);
    fn domain(&self) -> Domain;
    fn family(&self) -> &'static str;
    fn batch_key(&self) -> BatchKey;
    fn fingerprint(&self) -> u64;
    fn degeneracies(&self) -> Vec<Degeneracy<F>>;
    /// # Errors
    /// A report carrying where the solver stopped.
    fn closest(&self, p: Point<F>, seed: Option<UvPoint>, tol: Tolerance) -> Result<Closest<F>, Report>;
    fn analytic(&self, tol: Tolerance) -> Option<Analytic<F>>;
    fn triangles(&self) -> Option<&dyn TriangleView<F>>;
    fn specialised(&self, other: &dyn Surface<F>, tol: Tolerance) -> Option<Result<Intersection<F>, Report>>;
}

/// The one place the family trait becomes the object trait.
impl<F: Frame, S: Parametric<F>> Surface<F> for S {
    fn point(&self, uv: UvPoint) -> Point<F> {
        self.eval_point::<f64>(uv)
    }
    fn first(&self, uv: UvPoint) -> Jet1<F> {
        self.eval_first::<f64>(uv)
    }
    fn second(&self, uv: UvPoint) -> Jet2<F> {
        self.eval_second::<f64>(uv)
    }
    fn points(&self, uv: &[UvPoint], out: &mut Vec<Point<F>>) {
        let mm = Length::from_millimetres;
        let mut chunks = uv.chunks_exact(4);
        for c in &mut chunks {
            let u = Lanes::<4>::new([c[0].u(), c[1].u(), c[2].u(), c[3].u()]);
            let v = Lanes::<4>::new([c[0].v(), c[1].v(), c[2].v(), c[3].v()]);
            let p = self.eval_point(UvPoint::new(u, v));
            let (x, y, z) = (p.x().millimetres().to_array(), p.y().millimetres().to_array(), p.z().millimetres().to_array());
            out.extend((0..4).map(|i| Point::new(mm(x[i]), mm(y[i]), mm(z[i]))));
        }
        out.extend(chunks.remainder().iter().map(|q| self.eval_point::<f64>(*q)));
    }
    fn domain(&self) -> Domain {
        Parametric::domain(self)
    }
    fn family(&self) -> &'static str {
        Parametric::family(self)
    }
    fn batch_key(&self) -> BatchKey {
        Parametric::batch_key(self)
    }
    fn fingerprint(&self) -> u64 {
        Parametric::fingerprint(self)
    }
    fn degeneracies(&self) -> Vec<Degeneracy<F>> {
        Parametric::degeneracies(self)
    }
    fn closest(&self, p: Point<F>, seed: Option<UvPoint>, tol: Tolerance) -> Result<Closest<F>, Report> {
        Parametric::closest(self, p, seed, tol)
    }
    fn analytic(&self, tol: Tolerance) -> Option<Analytic<F>> {
        Parametric::analytic(self, tol)
    }
    fn triangles(&self) -> Option<&dyn TriangleView<F>> {
        Parametric::triangles(self)
    }
    fn specialised(&self, other: &dyn Surface<F>, tol: Tolerance) -> Option<Result<Intersection<F>, Report>> {
        Parametric::specialised(self, other, tol)
    }
}

/// One way of intersecting a pair; `None` means it does not apply. The list's order is the only ordered choice in the design.
pub trait PairMethod<F: Frame>: Send + Sync {
    fn name(&self) -> &'static str;
    fn attempt(&self, a: &dyn Surface<F>, b: &dyn Surface<F>, tol: Tolerance) -> Option<Result<Intersection<F>, Report>>;
}

pub struct MethodList<F: Frame> {
    pub methods: Vec<Box<dyn PairMethod<F>>>,
}

impl<F: Frame> MethodList<F> {
    /// The pair is put in a canonical order first, so that the result does not depend on argument order.
    pub fn intersect(&self, a: &dyn Surface<F>, b: &dyn Surface<F>, tol: Tolerance) -> Option<Result<Intersection<F>, Report>> {
        let key = |s: &dyn Surface<F>| (s.family(), s.batch_key(), s.fingerprint());
        let (x, y) = if key(a) <= key(b) { (a, b) } else { (b, a) };
        self.methods.iter().find_map(|m| m.attempt(x, y, tol))
    }
}

/// One family's storage: homogeneous inside, so a column can run lanes across instances without a downcast.
pub trait Column<F: Frame>: Send + Sync {
    fn family(&self) -> &'static str;
    fn surface(&self, index: u32) -> &dyn Surface<F>;
    fn points(&self, queries: &[ColumnQuery], out: &mut [Point<F>]);
}

#[derive(Clone, Copy, Debug)]
pub struct ColumnQuery {
    pub index: u32,
    pub uv: UvPoint,
    pub slot: u32,
}
```

The `Curve` seam is the same two layers, `ParametricCurve<F>` and `Curve<F>`.

**Where each dispatch form is acceptable.**

| Form | Where | Why |
|---|---|---|
| `dyn Surface`, per point | Everywhere that crosses a family boundary, at any derivative order | 0.9 to 1.6 ns at orders 0 and 1 on curved surfaces; 4 to 9 ns at order 2 |
| `dyn Surface`, per slice | Bulk evaluation where a family's lanes or shared allocation gain, in slices of 4 or more | Gains 7 to 13% on the cylinder and the shared allocation on heap-path NURBS. Costs 6 to 21% on bicubic and mesh. Never in slices of one: 9 to 23% worse |
| Closed enum, inside a family | Exact-form match; anything that wants exhaustiveness | About the cost of a virtual call; `dyn` is 10% slower when kinds are shuffled |
| Generics over `Parametric` and `Real` | Inside a family and its lane kernels; tight loops over one concrete type | The only route to lanes; 2.8 times cheaper for second derivatives on a plane |

**What could not be expressed.** Items 1 to 4 are each shown by a test that passes, and item 5 by a recorded compiler
error.

1. A closed form that needs private data on both sides. `specialised` sees `self` privately and `other` only as `dyn
   Surface`, so two extrusions that meet along exact lines go to the general method
   (`a_closed_form_that_needs_private_data_on_both_sides_cannot_be_expressed`). The way out is another question on the
   seam, so the list of questions is a central list of *representations*, though not of families.
2. A third derivative. `Offset` needs the base's derivative one order higher than it gives, so its `eval_second` is a
   central difference: exact to 1e-9 at order 1 and only to about 1e-5 at order 2
   (`an_offset_is_exact_in_position_and_first_derivative_and_approximate_in_the_second`).
3. A declared degeneracy in the lane path. `Real` has no comparison or selection, so `eval_*` cannot consult one: at a
   NURBS pole an offset computes a wrong or NaN position while the scalar `normal` knows better
   (`an_offset_at_a_degenerate_point_is_only_as_good_as_rounding_makes_it`). A NURBS sphere can declare the pole but
   cannot say the normal there (`a_pole_is_a_declared_degeneracy_in_both_families`).
4. A failure per lane or per batch item. `eval_*` is total and gives NaN; `checked_points` scans afterwards and reports
   only the first bad index (`a_mesh_hole_is_undefined_and_the_batch_reports_where`). L6 asks for a result per item,
   which this seam gets only by scanning.
5. An object-only family generic over its frame. Implementing `Surface` next to the blanket impl gives `E0119`, because
   a downstream crate could implement `Parametric` for it (compiled in `results/compile-alternatives.txt`), so
   `DynExtrusion` is pinned to `World`. It also gets no lanes, since a `Box<dyn Curve>` has no generic `eval_point`.

**What felt forced.**

- `Surface::analytic` names one family's enum in the cross-family trait. This is deliberate, as that enum is the
  vocabulary of exact forms, but it is an asymmetry, not an open seam.
- `analytic()` presents the carrier, not the patch: a bilinear patch answers an unbounded plane, so closed forms return
  unbounded curves and clipping belongs to the caller. And it is opt-in: a rational cylinder, which is exactly a
  cylinder, goes to the general method because no recogniser exists for it.
- `Code` is closed and has nothing for geometry, so every failure here is `Code::NotFinite` with the real kind as a
  number. `EntityRef` has no surface without topology, so a fingerprint stands in as a face.
- `Vector` is three lengths, which is wrong for a unit axis and for a derivative, and `Role` is sealed, so `Direction`
  and `Tangent` are newtypes here. Parameters are bare numbers: `normal_from_derivatives` compares a parameter with
  `tol.angle().radians()` even on a cone, where `v` is a length (`spikes/s4/src/seam.rs`, line 538).
- The blanket impl forwards 16 of `Parametric`'s methods by hand and adds the three slice methods, and curves repeat it.
- Above degree 3 there is no caller-supplied workspace, so every evaluation allocates (370 ns for degree 5 by 4).
- `FamilyName` is a bare string that the canonical pair order depends on, and nothing stops two families from choosing
  the same one.
- The store is built once by moving columns in, with nothing added afterwards, and only `points` runs lanes in the
  analytic column (`firsts` goes per call).

## What this does not show

- Anything about real SIMD. `Lanes<W>` is an array with per-element operations and whatever the compiler autovectorises.
  `fearless_simd` was not tried, and nothing here says what `proving-ground.p4` or `s5` will find.
- wasm, ARM, or any CPU but this one. Threads were out of scope.
- That a generic build and a v3 build give the same bits. Lane widths agree within each build; the two builds were not
  comparable, as above.
- Whether batching across instances can pay. The store's analytic cost was not broken down.
- That the general intersection is any good. It is a stand-in, chosen to use only the public interface;
  `proving-ground.p5` decides the real method.
- Trimming, topology, multi-patch surfaces, sweeps or blends. The families are the ones listed above.

## What it decides

For `proving-ground.design` to confirm:

1. The geometry seam is two layers, with the family trait generic over `Real` and the object trait dyn-compatible,
   joined by one blanket impl. Dispatch cost does not constrain where it sits.
2. Method choice is an ordered list of methods asking surfaces what they can present, not a table of pairs or a visitor.
3. Anything that depends only on the surface is computed at construction, never inside `eval`. The cone shows what it
   costs.
4. Families live in columns from the start, because homogeneous data cannot be recovered from `dyn Surface` without a
   downcast. Sorting by kind is the part of L29 proven here, and the store still has to earn its place: this one is 2.6
   times worse than per-call on analytic surfaces.
5. `occam-units` needs a unit axis and a per-parameter derivative, and probably typed parameters. `occam-report` needs
   geometry codes and a way to name a surface with no topology. Both should be settled before the seam freezes.
6. The seam carries lanes and they are bit-identical, but no speed from lanes should be assumed until `Real` can compare
   and select or `p4` shows otherwise.

## Open questions for the user

1. **Is anything in scope that needs an exact third derivative?** Offset curvature and curvature-continuous blends
   would. The fixed ladder of position, first and second is the choice that is hardest to change after the seam freezes.
   If yes, it needs a third rung or a jet of chosen order. My recommendation is to keep two and accept differencing
   unless a named feature needs more.

Not for the user but for `proving-ground.s5`, which answers the contents of `occam-math`: whether `Real` should gain
comparison and selection. Degeneracies in lanes, and the lane-by-lane knot spans and point location above, point at it.
Comparison is exact in IEEE 754, so it looks compatible with the determinism contract, but this spike did not try it.
