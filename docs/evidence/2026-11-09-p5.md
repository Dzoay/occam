# `proving-ground.p5`: which intersection method?

Spike on the never-merged `spike-p5` branch, 2026-11-09. Code is in `spikes/p5/`, raw output in `spikes/p5/results/`, the
commands in `spikes/p5/measure.sh`, and `spikes/p5/summarise.py` rebuilds the tables.

## Question

For each family of surface pair, which method should `occam-intersect` use: closed forms, marching, subdivision (Bézier
clipping or similar) or a hybrid? And does [L29](2026-11-08-phasekit-lessons.md) hold: "Batch surfaces by kind and degree.
Keep intersection marching scalar, but parallel across curves"?

## What was measured

**Methods.** All are implemented in `spikes/p5/src/`.

| Name | Method |
|---|---|
| `closed` | Closed forms: plane with plane, sphere, cylinder and cone; sphere with sphere; cylinder with cylinder, with the coaxial, parallel and tangent cases. |
| `march` | A 16-cell lattice plus a boundary scan for start points, then a tangent predictor and Newton corrector, with step control and loop closure. An analytic side is used in implicit form. Scalar, in rounds of 32 traces across a thread pool. `marchN` is the same with an N-cell lattice. |
| `subdiv` | A breadth-first traversal of Bézier patch pairs. Pairs are rejected by k-DOP bounds and by each patch's control points against the other's fat plane, in lane kernels. Leaves are resolved by Newton on patch edges, and the crossings are linked into chains. |
| `subclip` | `subdiv` plus the range-reduction step of Bézier clipping. |
| `hybrid` | The same traversal with coarser leaves, giving one start point per leaf, then `march`'s tracer. |
| `hybclip` | `hybrid` with range reduction in its traversal. |
| `auto` | `closed` where the pair has a closed form, otherwise `hybrid`. |

**Cases.** There are 110 surface pairs whose answers are known by construction, all under a rigid placement so that none
is axis-aligned. The answers come from elementary geometry or from independent one-dimensional scans of a known level-set
function, not from any method under test.

| Group | Cases | Contents |
|---|---|---|
| Analytic pairs | 37 | Every closed-form family and its degenerate cases, plus 3 pairs with no closed form here (sphere with cylinder, cone with cylinder) |
| Analytic with spline | 9 | Planes and cylinders against polynomial and exact rational surfaces, including branch points and a Viviani node |
| Spline pairs | 6 | Double-well graphs, exact rational cylinders with seams |
| Near-tangent sweep | 48 | Four families at gap `eps` from 1e-1 to 1e-12 (loop radius about `sqrt(eps)`), with length scale 4 |
| Small features | 7 | One bicubic bump cut 1e-2 to 1e-8 below its peak |
| Many loops | 3 | One bicubic surface and a plane, with 25, 169 and 625 truth components (17, 145 and 577 curves) |

**Scoring** (`src/eval.rs`). A true component is found if 98% of its samples lie within tolerance of the result, where the
tolerance is the smaller of `2e-3 × scale` and a fifth of the component's diameter. A result is *correct* if everything is
found, nothing is spurious and it has no more pieces than the answer allows. A *silent* failure is a wrong answer with no
failure report. A *reported* failure is a wrong answer that says so. Every returned vertex is measured against both surfaces.

**Protocol.** Intel Core i7-8700K, 6 physical cores, `powersave` governor (so speed-ups read a little low), rustc 1.99.0,
`x86_64-unknown-linux-gnu`, empty `RUSTFLAGS`. A second build with `-C target-cpu=haswell` was used only to compare lane
widths. Runs were pinned with `taskset -c 0-(n-1)`, 7 repeats, minimum and median. Exceptions are listed under "What this does
not show".

**Commits.** Correctness, lanes and lattice sizes are from `7e66c0b`. The hybrid and marching speed-ups are from later commits
that only cached seed points (`dcc0b6a`) and moved the output hash out of the timed region (`b3328ed`). Output digests were
unchanged across those commits, so the correctness results carry over.

**Sources.** Authors, years and venues were checked against Crossref and library records on 2026-11-09. None was opened
during the spike, so what each is cited for rests on memory.

- Closed forms: Patrikalakis and Maekawa, *Shape Interrogation for Computer Aided Design and
  Manufacturing* (2002), ch. 5; Sarraga (1983); Levin (1976,
  1979).
- Marching: Bajaj, Hoffmann, Lynch and Hopcroft (1988); Barnhill, Farin, Jordan and Piper (1987) for lattice start points.
- Subdivision: Lane and Riesenfeld (1980); Houghton et al. (1985); Sederberg and Nishita (1990) for Bézier clipping;
  Klosowski et al. (1998) for k-DOPs.
- Hybrid: Barnhill and Kersey (1990).
- Rational forms and knot insertion: Piegl and Tiller, *The NURBS Book*; Boehm (1980); Cohen, Lyche and Riesenfeld (1980).
- Contact detection: von Neumann's alternating projections.

### Which failures are silent

- **`march` never reported a failure on this set: all 13 wrong answers are silent.** Three are tangent contacts
  (`ss_tangent_ext`, `ss_tangent_int`, and `cc_parallel_tangent`, tangent along a whole line). Eight are near-tangent
  sweep cases, listed below. Two are dense features: on the 169-component surface it returned 144 of 145 curves, and on the
  625-component surface **256 of 577**.
- **`hybrid` has 2 silent failures, both the same kind:** at `eps = 1e-10` a loop of radius 1e-5 comes back as one contact
  point. The position error is bounded by the loop's size, but it is still the wrong shape of answer. Its 4 reported failures
  are the same two families at `eps` 1e-11 and 1e-12.
- **`auto`'s one silent failure** is `nt_poly_loop` at `eps = 1e-10`.
- **Pure subdivision's failure flag is not usable as a signal.** `subclip` raised it on 58 of 110 cases and 45 of those were
  correct, so 78% of its reports were false alarms. `hybrid` raised it on 7, with 3 false alarms.

Near-tangent sweep, for `eps` = 1e-1 to 1e-12, left to right (`.` correct, `S` silent, `R` reported, `-` not applicable):

| Family | `closed` | `march` | `subclip` | `hybrid` |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Two spheres (loop) | `............` | `.........SSS` | `.........SSS` | `.........SRR` |
| Plane and cylinder (two lines) | `............` | `............` | `............` | `............` |
| Polynomial pair (loop) | `------------` | `.........SSS` | `....R..RSSSS` | `.........SRR` |
| Polynomial pair (two lines) | `------------` | `..........SS` | `...RRRRRRRRR` | `............` |

Small isolated features did not defeat the lattice. All seven bump cases were found at 16 cells, down to a cut 1e-8 below
the peak, because Newton's reach of two cells brings a lone feature in. I expected the opposite. What defeats a lattice is
*density*, as the next table shows.

### Can a denser lattice replace subdivision?

On the 625-component surface, 577 true curves, at one thread:

| Start points | Components fully covered | Curves | Reported? | Time (ms) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Lattice 16 | 49 of 625 | 256 | no, silent | 24 |
| Lattice 64 | 617 | 573 | yes | 264 |
| Lattice 256 | 621 | 575 | yes | 702 |
| Lattice 1024 | 625 | 577 | correct | 2716 |
| Hybrid | 625 | 577 | correct | 1064 |

The lattice needed depends on a feature density that is not known in advance, and the hybrid needs no such guess. On the
169-component surface a 64-cell lattice was enough. These times are from a separate part from the speed table below, which
measured `march256` at 630 ms.

The closed forms take about 170 µs per pair, including sampling polylines of up to 8000 points. The `subclip` and `subdiv`
rows, from `7e66c0b`, still hash the output inside the timed region. Those workloads return few points, so the effect should
be small, but I did not measure it. On the closed forms, which return dense polylines, moving the hash out raised the 6-thread
speed-up from 2.55 to 4.59 times.

**Range reduction transformed pure subdivision and did nothing for the hybrid.** On the spline pairs `subclip` took 256 ms
against `subdiv`'s 4427 ms, 17 times faster, with 6 more cases correct and no case made worse. `hybclip` took 109 ms against
`hybrid`'s 111 ms on the same commit. I had written in a code comment that range reduction would not help because a
surface/surface solution is a curve. Measuring showed that to be wrong for pure subdivision.

The hybrid's time is mostly tracing and filtering start points. On the 577-curve pair the traversal's kernels took 74 ms and
leaf work 47 ms of 1064 ms.

### Lanes

Kernels are written on `occam_math::Lanes<W>`: splitting and bounds are `W` patches at a time, pair tests `W` pairs, slab tests
`W` control points. The patches of a surface share one degree, so batching needs no sorting.

| Single thread | W = 1 | W = 2 | W = 4 |
|---|---|---|---|
| `subdiv`, 25-component surface: whole run (ms) | 2606.6 | 2585.8 | 2588.1 |
| of which lane kernels (ms) | 109.5 | 87.3 | 91.8 |
| Same, `target-cpu=haswell`: kernels (ms) | 121.1 | 103.8 | 99.4 |
| `hybrid`, 15 spline pairs: whole run (ms) | 114.3 | 112.1 | 111.3 |
| of which lane kernels (ms) | 15.7 | 13.2 | 13.4 |
| Lane fill, `subdiv` / `hybrid` | 1.000 / 1.000 | 1.000 / 0.995 | 0.998 / 0.979 |

Lanes speed the kernels by 1.17 to 1.25 times, but the kernels are only **3.5%** of `subdiv` and **12%** of `hybrid`, so in
the baseline build a whole run changes by 0.7% and 2.6% between widths 1 and 4. Fill is not the limit. Amdahl is. Clipping's range reduction is scalar, and it is where
the large gain came from.

To bound what lanes could do for marching, I wrote a lane kernel for its unit of work: evaluate a surface at `W` points on
`W` different patches, which is what curves marched in lockstep would need.

| Nanoseconds per evaluation | `Nurbs::eval` | W = 1 | W = 2 | W = 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Baseline build | 193.7 | 172.6 | 181.9 | 221.8 |
| `target-cpu=haswell` | 201.9 | 165.5 | 168.7 | 202.2 |

**Four lanes are 22 to 28% slower than one.** The patch search and control-point gather are per lane, and the gather
costs more than the arithmetic saves.

### Determinism

No row has more than one digest, across 1, 2, 4 and 6 threads, across lane widths 1, 2 and 4, between the baseline and
`haswell` builds, or across the commits above. The digest covers every returned point and the failure flag.

### Failure modes found while building it

Each of these was a real bug, and each is a test the real implementation should start with:

- A hull test without a rounding margin rejected a plane exactly tangent to a sphere.
- A curve through a corner shared by both patches' subdivisions gives a leaf one crossing. Dyadic splits make this common.
- A corrector can jump to a neighbouring loop. The trace then never returns to its start and winds until the step limit.
- Duplicate detection needs a tube as wide as the chord's sag, and near tangency as wide as `sqrt(tolerance)`.
- A seam of a closed NURBS is a domain edge. A sphere's pole is too, and a contact there looks like the curve ending.
- A double cone's lower nappe reverses the radial direction when inverting a point to parameters.
- Newton loses rank at a tangency. Alternating closest-point projection does not.
- Serial bookkeeping on start points held the hybrid to 1.2 times at 6 threads, until the points were cached and filtered in
  parallel (3.0 to 5.2 times, with identical output).

## Answer

| Pair | Method | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Analytic pair with a closed form | **Closed form** | 58 of 58 correct, within 1e-12, about 170 µs per pair |
| Analytic pair with no closed form, analytic with spline, spline with spline | **Hybrid:** subdivision for start points, then marching | 104 of 110 overall and 2 silent failures, against 13 silent for marching alone |

Do not use lattice-seeded marching on its own. It is the cheapest method, and it returned 256 of 577 curves with no warning.
Do not use pure subdivision as the main method: even with clipping it is wrong on 21 of 110, and its failure flag is mostly
false alarms.

**L29 holds in part.**

- *"Keep marching scalar"* holds. Lane evaluation across curves is slower than scalar at width 4.
- *"Parallel across curves"* holds. Tracing scales 3.0 to 5.2 times at 6 threads, with identical output, using rounds of a fixed
  size merged by index.
- *"Batch surfaces by kind and degree"* is true but is not worth much for intersection. Batching works, with lane fill at or
  above 0.979, and it buys under 3% of a run. The algorithmic choices matter far more: range reduction was worth 17 times on
  pure subdivision, and the choice of start points decides whether curves are found at all.

## What this does not show

- **The correctness counts are in-sample.** The same 110 cases were used to find and fix the bugs above, and the leaf sizes,
  budgets and contact thresholds were set while watching them. There is no held-out set, so treat the counts as upper bounds.
- **Two test definitions changed during the work.** `cc_oblique` got a taller cylinder because its truth was untrimmed, and
  `nn_cc_two_loops` allows 4 arcs rather than 2 because a seam cuts each loop.
- There are no trimmed faces, no real CAD NURBS, and no use inside a boolean. Surfaces have one degree each, and subdivision
  asserts that. Only 3 pairs are analytic with no closed form.
- Branch points are passed through and returned as arcs, not classified.
- **Lanes were measured only on portable `Lanes<W>` at widths up to 4.** Explicit SIMD through `fearless_simd`, AVX-512 and
  wasm were not measured, so "lanes do not help" means "not this way, at these widths".
- Results are from one machine. One-thread times varied by up to 15% between measurement parts: the 15-spline `hybrid` row was
  110.9 ms in one part and 127.5 ms in another, so read the speed-ups as approximate.
- **Protocol departures.** `subdiv` on the spline pairs and `subclip` on the 577-curve pair have 3 repeats, not 7. The 16-cell
  marching rows on the many-loop surfaces take 18 to 71 ms at one thread, under the 100 ms minimum, so denser-lattice rows
  are reported in their place.
- **Minimum and median differ by more than 5% in several rows, and I did not establish why.** They are, with the worst thread
  count: `spline_set march` 11.8%, the 577-curve `hybrid` 10.2%, `march64` 9.7%, `hybclip` 7.3%, the NURBS-plane
  `hybrid` 7.1%, the 16-cell `march` on the 145- and 577-curve pairs 7.1% and 6.6%, `spline_set hybrid` and `subclip` 6.3%,
  and `march256` on the 145-curve pair 5.3%. One marching baseline measured 1319 ms when the
  true value is about 625 ms. A diagnostic (`results/diag.txt`) ruled out CPU 0 and allocator trimming for that row, and it was
  re-measured, so a transient disturbance on the machine is likely but unproven.
- I did not establish why `march` on the 15 spline pairs scales only 2.22 times at 6 threads.
- Memory use was not measured. Subdivision is bounded by a budget, and exceeding it is a reported failure.

## What it decides

For `occam-intersect` and `proving-ground.design`:

1. Closed forms come first, then the hybrid. Lattice seeding is not a route to completeness.
2. A failure must be part of the result: a stalled trace, an exhausted budget, an unresolved leaf. Marching never reported one
   of its 13 wrong answers here.
3. Rejection bounds carry a rounding margin.
4. Threads go across pairs and curves, in fixed-size rounds merged by index. Marching stays scalar.
5. Range reduction is worth having only if a pure-subdivision path is kept. The hybrid does not need it.
6. If lane kernels are kept for traversal, `occam_math::Lanes` needs `min`, `max` and comparisons, which the spike had to build
   through `to_array`. On this evidence that is low priority.
7. The list of failure modes above seeds the test-first rewrite.

## Open questions for the user

**Below the resolvable size, should a tiny loop be returned as a contact point or as a reported failure?** Today the hybrid
returns a contact point for a loop of radius about 1e-5 at scale 4, with no warning. That is close in position and wrong in
kind. I recommend a reported result that says "contact or loop smaller than `d`", so that nothing downstream mistakes it for a
true tangency.
