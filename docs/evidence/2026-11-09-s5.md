# `proving-ground.s5`: the maths contract

Evidence for `proving-ground.design`. Code, raw results and the commands that produced them are in `spikes/s5/` on the
`spike-s5` branch; `spikes/s5/results/commands.txt` lists them, and `tools/comparetab.py` and `tools/benchtab.py` turn
the raw files into the tables below (`results/tables-compare.txt`, `results/tables-speed.txt`). The sources that were
measured are commit `576f905`; nothing under `src/` or `shim/` has changed since.

**Provenance of the runs.** The machine controlling the runs crashed part-way. Everything that had finished before that
is kept as written, and the steps it interrupted (node with simd128, the decisions, the lane check, code size, folding
and the tunable check) were run again afterwards from the same sources.

## Question

What really needs to be identical on native and wasm, which of `sin`, `cos`, `atan2`, `acos` and others that means occam
must own, and what owning them costs against std. Computing the same model in the browser and on a server is the case
that would need it ([the user's decision](../decisions/2026-11-09-one-maths-choke-point.md)). The answer is the contents
of `occam-math` (L23). `gates determinism` already holds `+ - * / sqrt`, so this is about everything else.

## What was measured

**Machine.** Intel Core i7-8700K, 6 physical cores, governor `powersave`, one thread pinned to one physical core.
`rustc 1.99.0 (b940084d7 2026-09-28)`, glibc 2.44, wasmtime 49.0.2, node v26.13.0, Python 3.14.9. Release builds are
`opt-level = 3`, fat LTO, one codegen unit, with no `RUSTFLAGS` unless a table says otherwise.

**The implementations of std.** `x86_64-unknown-linux-gnu`, which calls the system's glibc; the same binary with
`GLIBC_TUNABLES` hiding AVX2 and FMA; `x86_64-unknown-linux-musl`; `wasm32-wasip2` under wasmtime; and
`wasm32-unknown-unknown` under node, as a module with no imports, so its maths is pure wasm.

**Inputs.** 165 pairs of function and input set, 1,048,576 inputs each, built only from integers and IEEE arithmetic so
that every target sees the same bits. The sets cover uniform ranges, multiples of pi/2 give or take 1,024 ulps, the
angles `i * 2 pi / n` that a pattern of up to 720 items produces, values near 1, tiny and huge values, small-integer
`atan2` ratios, and special values. Each call goes through a function pointer on `black_box`ed values, so nothing folds.
A separate experiment shows what folding does.

**Reference.** Python's `decimal` and `fractions`, with no installed packages: each value computed to at least 120
digits and rounded to the nearest double, on the first 20,000 inputs of each set, for twelve functions. It was checked
against published values of those functions and of `sin(1e22)`.

**Decisions.** Twelve small decisions a kernel makes, 200,000 cases each, eleven of them on positions that are
degenerate by construction: tangent circles from a bolt circle, a tangent line, a point taken around a circle and back
to the seam, collinear points, a point on a circle, a circle's segment count. Three kinds of difference feed them: the
real targets; `std` against the owned functions; and a `std` with every `sin`, `cos`, `atan2` and `acos` result moved by
one ulp, which is harsher than any real pair of libraries. Code: `spikes/s5/src/decide.rs`.

**Candidates for owning.** `sin`, `cos`, `sin_cos`, `atan2`, and `tan`, `atan`, `asin`, `acos`, `hypot` derived from
them or from `sqrt`, written once over `Real` and `Lanes<W>`, using only `+ - * / sqrt` and integer bit operations.
Reduction is Cody and Waite (1980) below 1.6e6 and Payne and Hanek above it; the polynomial kernels and the `atan`
coefficients are fdlibm's (Sun Microsystems, a permissive notice that must be preserved, see the open questions), and
the double-word steps are Shewchuk's (1997). A second implementation in Python floats and integers, sharing no code with
the Rust, checks the bits (`tools/own_check.py`).

**Speed.** Eight configurations, nine runs each of at least 120 ms, 4,096 elements per pass, inputs uniform in `[-2 pi,
2 pi]` or `[-1, 1]`, reported as the median in nanoseconds per element. Minimum and median differ by under 5%
everywhere.

## Results

### 1. Do they differ, and by how much?

Yes. Share of inputs whose bits differ, and the largest distance in ulps. Every row covers 3 to 6 million inputs.

|---|---|---|---|
| `sin` | 2.09% / 1 | 2.09% / 1 | 0 |
| `cos` | 2.04% / 1 | 2.04% / 1 | 0 |
| `tan` | 2.49% / 1 | 2.49% / 1 | 0 |
| `atan` | 1.13% / 1 | 1.13% / 1 | 0 |
| `atan2` | 11.69% / 1 | 11.69% / 1 | 0 |
| `asin` | 2.51% / 1 | 2.51% / 1 | 0 |
| `acos` | 2.78% / 1 | 2.78% / 1 | 0 |
| `exp` | 0.08% / 1 | 6.83% / 1 | 6.75% / 1 |
| `ln` | 0.002% / 1 | 2.45% / 1 | 2.45% / 1 |
| `powf` | 0.07% / 1 | 9.00% / 1 | 8.94% / 1 |
| `hypot` | 1.94% / 1 | 6.20% / 1 | 6.52% / 1 |
| `cbrt` | 7.26% / 1 | 0 | 7.26% / 1 |

- **musl and wasip2 agree on every bit** of all 165 pairs, so wasip2's numbers are musl's. The two wasm targets are not
  one library: they disagree on `exp`, `ln`, `powf`, `hypot`, `cbrt`, `log2`, `exp2`, `cosh`, `asinh` and `acosh`.
- **Where the disagreement is:** `sin` differs on 3.1% of ordinary angles, including 3.15% of the pattern angles, and on
  none of the tiny inputs or those next to a multiple of pi/2. `atan2` differs on 17.7% of `y/x` from small integers.
- **Other functions:** `sinh`, `cosh`, `asinh`, `acosh`, `log10` and `tanh` reach 2 ulps, at 2% to 22%. `exp_m1`,
  `ln_1p`, `atanh`, `exp2` and `log2` differ on under 0.1% for glibc against wasip2.
- **`sin_cos`** gives exactly `sin` and `cos` on every build, with no mismatch on any input.
- No function, on any pair, has a NaN where the other has a number, or a different sign of zero. NaN sign and payload
  are not compared, because `canonical_bits` removes them.

### 3. What needs them?

The table is analysis, from how each operation is defined. The experiment below tests the part that can be tested.

| What the kernel computes | Functions it needs | Can a last-bit difference change a discrete outcome? |
|---|---|---|
| NURBS and Bézier evaluation, derivatives, knot insertion, degree elevation, clipping | none: `+ - * /` | no: the bits do not differ |
| Plane and line evaluation, normals, unit vectors | `sqrt` at most | no: identical |
| Rigid transforms and placements | `sin`, `cos` once per rotation, then `+ - *` | coordinates move by last-bit amounts; decisions only as in the rows below |
| Circle, ellipse, cylinder, cone, sphere, torus evaluation and derivatives | `sin_cos`, in bulk | a coordinate moves within tolerance |
| Inverse evaluation on those surfaces | `atan2`, and `asin` or `atan2` for latitude | a parameter moves in the last bits; the side of a seam is the sign of a noise-sized value, so it is discrete |
| Analytic intersections of plane, cylinder, cone, sphere | `sqrt`, `atan2`, `sin`, `cos`, a little `acos` | tangent, crossing or missing is discrete: stable inside a band, flips on noise without one. Near tangency a root is the square root of a tiny difference, so a coordinate can move far more than a last bit |
| Cone-torus and torus-torus, cubics and quartics | `cbrt` or `acos` and `cos` in closed forms, or Newton with `+ - * /` only | the order of near-equal roots, and the same amplification. Newton avoids `cbrt` |
| General surface intersection by marching | what evaluation needs | step counts and branches, through evaluation and tolerance tests |
| Point classification, orientation, predicates | none of their own: they inherit their inputs | a test with no tolerance on a degenerate input flips on any last-bit change; the same test inside a band does not |
| Tessellating circles and arcs | `acos` or `atan2` for the count, `sin_cos` for points, `ceil` | a count sitting on an integer can change the mesh |
| Fillets, chamfers, draft, offsets | `sin`, `cos`, `tan`, `atan2`, `acos` | as for intersections and evaluation |
| Mass properties | constants, and `sin`, `cos` for arcs | values in the last bits only |
| Dihedral angles | `atan2` or `acos` | a threshold test: discrete only within a last bit of the threshold |
| Hyperbolas, law curves, gradients | `sinh`, `cosh`, `exp`, `ln`, `powf`, only if a curve kind needs them | not used by a B-rep's core geometry |

None of this needs `exp`, `ln` or `powf`. `cbrt` is needed only if a closed-form cubic is chosen over Newton.

### 4. What would owning cost?

**Bits.** The owned functions gave the same bits on all five builds of the sweep, 51 million owned values each, and at
lane widths 1, 2, 4 and 8 against the scalar code on six builds (baseline, AVX2 and FMA, Haswell, musl, wasip2 and
wasip2 with simd128), in debug as in release (`results/lanes.txt`). The Python reimplementation of `sin`, `cos` and
`atan2` reproduces them on 360,000 values from each of four builds (`results/own-check.txt`), so a recorded hash from a
second implementation is possible, as `gates determinism` requires. The speed runs' digests of owned workloads also
agree across all eight configurations.

**Speed.** Median nanoseconds per element. "Own" is the generic code at the lane width shown. std has no vector form.

| | x86-64 baseline: std | own, 2 lanes | AVX2 and FMA: std | own, 4 lanes | wasip2 simd128: std | own, 2 lanes | node simd128: std | own, 2 lanes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `sin` | 10.7 | 7.4 | 10.6 | 4.1 | 14.3 | 8.4 | 12.9 | 8.8 |
| `cos` | 11.3 | 7.3 | 11.5 | 3.9 | 13.9 | 7.9 | 13.2 | 8.6 |
| `sin_cos` | 16.9 | 8.0 | 16.6 | 4.3 | 22.8 | 9.0 | 22.9 | 9.1 |
| `tan` | 11.0 | 13.4 | 10.9 | 10.6 | 19.7 | 16.7 | 19.4 | 17.1 |
| `atan` | 6.4 | 6.1 | 6.4 | 3.3 | 10.1 | 6.5 | 11.7 | 8.4 |
| `atan2` | 18.9 | 7.4 | 19.0 | 4.1 | 22.4 | 8.1 | 21.7 | 10.4 |
| `asin` | 10.1 | 9.1 | 10.0 | 5.1 | 9.0 | 9.4 | 11.8 | 11.9 |
| `acos` | 9.8 | 9.2 | 9.8 | 5.0 | 8.0 | 9.4 | 9.2 | 11.7 |
| `hypot` | 5.1 | 2.1 | 5.2 | 1.1 | 9.4 | 2.0 | 52.7 | 2.6 |

- The AVX2 and FMA columns are what occam gives up by building one baseline ([rule 5](../DETERMINISM.md#the-rules)).
  Baseline x86-64 gains nothing past two lanes, because SSE2 holds two doubles.
- The scalar owned `sin` and `cos` take 9.7 and 9.8 ns on x86-64 against std's 10.7 and 11.3, and 11.0 and 10.7 ns on
  baseline wasip2 against 13.9. Under baseline node they are 13.3 and 13.1 ns against 12.8 and 12.9, the one case where
  the scalar form is slower.
- Without simd128, lanes do not help on wasm, and the derived functions lose: `acos` is 20.9 ns against std's 8.0 on
  wasip2, and 25 to 38 ns against 9.4 on node. A kernel should not put `asin` or `acos` in a hot loop on baseline wasm.
  On x86-64 and on wasm with simd128 they are close to std.
- The speed of `tan` depends on what the autovectoriser does with each width (13.4, 10.5 and 8.6 ns at 2, 4 and 8 lanes
  on baseline x86-64), so wider is not always faster.

**Code size.** `sin`, `cos`, `atan2` and the four-lane `sin_cos` and `atan2`, as a release cdylib. On wasm the owned
module is 6,211 bytes against 10,439 for the three std functions alone (an empty module is 344), so owning is smaller.
On x86-64 it adds 6,538 bytes of `.text`; std's adds 386, because libm is linked dynamically.

**Accuracy.** In the second table: `sin`, `cos` and `atan` match musl, `atan2` is close to it, and the derived `tan`,
`asin` and `acos` are worse, at up to 1.83 ulps.

### 5. Other sources of difference

**Constant folding is a real one.** The same `x.sin()` was compiled with a literal argument and with the argument hidden
behind `black_box`, on the inputs where glibc and wasip2 disagree.

| build | `sin`, `cos`, `tan`, `atan`, `atan2`, `asin`, `acos`, `exp`, `ln`, `powf` | `cbrt`, `hypot` |
|---|---|---|
| x86-64, release and debug | literal and run time agree | agree |
| wasm32-wasip2, release | **every picked input differs** (2,372 of 2,372 in the ten functions) | agree |
| wasm32-wasip2, debug | literal and run time agree | agree |

In the wasm release build the literal's digest equals the x86-64 digest exactly: the compiler folded the call with the
build machine's glibc and put that value in the module. So in one wasm build the same source expression gives different
bits in release and in debug, and a build on another machine could give others. The owned functions cannot do this,
since folding plain IEEE arithmetic is exact.

**No difference found:** `min`, `max`, `%`, `floor`, `ceil`, `round`, `trunc`, conversion through `f32`, `to_degrees`,
`to_radians`, `powi` and `mul_add` on every pair of targets. `mul_add` is identical everywhere but costs 0.25 ns with
FMA, 2.1 ns on glibc's baseline, and 14 to 16 ns on musl and wasm. Subnormal inputs are in the `tiny` and `scales` sets
and no build treated them differently. `target-cpu=haswell` and `+avx2,+fma` left `plain a*b+c`, every std digest and
every owned digest unchanged.

**FMA at run time: no difference found.** `GLIBC_TUNABLES=glibc.cpu.hwcaps=-AVX2,-FMA,-FMA4,-AVX` does take effect: the
loader's platform changes from `haswell` to `x86_64`, the AVX, FMA and AVX2 active bits clear, and `cos` gets 17% slower
(11.3 to 13.3 ns), so libm switches code for at least that function (`results/tunables.txt`). `sin`, `tan`, `atan` and
`atan2` keep their speed. With the tunable set, the bits of all 173 million values in the sweep equal those of the
default run. I did not run on a CPU that lacks FMA.

## Answer

**Very little must be identical for the first release, and owning what matters is cheap.**

Nothing has to match between targets until the same model is computed in two places, which [the user has
said](../decisions/2026-11-09-one-maths-choke-point.md) is a likely later feature and not a first-release one. For the
functions a kernel uses, std's differences are a single ulp in 1% to 12% of calls. They flip decisions made with no
tolerance, which a kernel should not be making anyway, and the side of a seam that a point on it lands on.

But `std` cannot do two things the kernel wants, whichever way that decision goes:

- **It has no vector form.** Evaluating a cylinder, cone, sphere or torus in lanes needs a lane-generic `sin_cos`, and
  inverting one needs `atan2`. Looping over scalar `std` gives up 1.5x to 2.7x at baseline and on wasm with simd128.
- **Literal arguments fold with the build machine's libm**, which would break the contract's debug-and-release promise
  on wasm if `f64::sin` were allowed.

The owned `sin_cos` and `atan2` are faster than std at two or more lanes on x86-64 and on wasm with simd128. On baseline
wasm the scalar `sin` and `cos` are faster under wasmtime and within 4% of std under node. They are smaller on wasm,
match musl's accuracy for `sin` and `cos`, and are identical on every target and lane width tested.

**Own now, in `occam-math`:** `sin_cos` (and `sin` and `cos`, its two halves) and `atan2`. These are what analytic
evaluation and inverse evaluation run in bulk.

**Add when a caller first needs them, as thin derivations of those two:** `tan`, `atan`, `asin`, `acos`. `atan`, `asin` and
`acos` are slower than std on baseline wasm, up to 2.6x under wasmtime and 4x under node, and each of the four is at most
1.83 ulps.

**Leave banned:** `exp`, `ln`, `powf`, `exp2`, `exp_m1`, `ln_1p`, `log2`, `log10` and the hyperbolics: no core B-rep
operation needs them. `cbrt`: prefer Newton iteration on `+ - * /` for cubics. `hypot`: `(x * x + y * y).sqrt()` through
`Real` is already identical, and occam's lengths are far from where it overflows.

**`docs/DETERMINISM.md` should say:**

1. `occam_math` owns `sin_cos` and `atan2`, and the promise covers them: the same bits at every lane width, thread count
   and target, in debug and in release. `ci/determinism-hashes.txt` records them, from a separate implementation.
2. They are not correctly rounded. Their accuracy is stated and tested: `sin` and `cos` within about 0.75 ulps, `atan2`
   within about 1.5 on the samples. No caller may rely on more.
3. Every other transcendental stays banned until `occam-math` owns it. The ban holds for literal arguments too, since
   the compiler folds those with the machine that builds them.
4. A decision about computed geometry never compares a value with exactly zero. It uses a tolerance band, or a predicate
   on exact inputs. A band moves the decision boundary rather than removing it, and the seam's side and a segment count
   on an integer need a rule of their own.

## What this does not show

- **One CPU and one version of each library.** glibc 2.44 and an Intel 8700K only. No aarch64, no browser engine (node
  is V8, not a browser), no other wasm engine. A different glibc can change the numbers in tables 1 and 2.
- **Samples, not bounds.** The largest errors are the largest seen, on 20,000 inputs per set. Nothing here proves a
  bound. "Correctly rounded on this sample" is not "correctly rounded".
- **The decisions are synthetic.** They show the contrast between a band and no band. The rates depend on how the cases
  were built, and this is not a real kernel's operations.
- **Whether a folded value differs across build machines** was inferred from the digests, not measured by building on a
  second machine.
- **Payne and Hanek's path** is exercised only by the `huge` set.
- **Single-thread speed only**, on arrays of 4,096 uniform inputs, on one core under `powersave`. Threads were not part
  of this question.

## What it decides

For `proving-ground.design`:

- `occam-math` gains a lane-generic `sin_cos` and `atan2`, written test-first from the published methods. Everything
  else in `clippy.toml`'s ban list stays banned.
- `gates determinism` extends to them, with hashes from a separate implementation.
- A kernel-wide rule: decisions on computed values use a band or an exact predicate.

## Open questions for the user

1. **Owning `sin_cos` and `atan2` for the first release?** I recommend yes: it is the only way to get batch evaluation,
   and it is at least as fast as std except on baseline wasm under node, where the scalar form is up to 4% slower. The
   trade is that their accuracy is musl's, at about 0.74 and 1.40 ulps, where glibc's is about 0.5. The alternative is
   to stay on std, with scalar-only evaluation and bits that differ by target.
2. **Fdlibm's coefficients.** The candidates use fdlibm's published polynomial coefficients, under Sun's notice, which
   must be preserved. For the rewrite, either carry the notice in `LICENSES/`, or generate new coefficients with our own
   minimax fit. I recommend generating new ones, since the code is rewritten anyway, but this is a licensing decision.
3. **Promising browser-server identity.** It comes free for what `occam-math` owns, but holds only while every function
   a computation touches is owned. Should the contract promise it now, or say it holds for owned functions and leave the
   promise until that feature is real? I recommend the second.
